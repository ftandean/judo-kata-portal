Introduction

What is Kodomo-no-Kata?

Kodomo-no-Kata (こどもの形 / 講道館少年少女柔道基本形) was established by the Kodokan to cultivate proper etiquette, foundational movement, and physical confidence in children and beginners before entering competitive Randori.

The kata is systematically structured into Three Core Groups:
1. Reihō & Kihon-dōsa (Etiquette, Posture & Breakfalls): Mastering standing/seated bows, natural postures (Shizentai), and safe breakfalls (Ukemi).
2. Nage-waza (Foundational Throwing Mechanics): Practicing the three fundamental throwing categories — foot sweeps (Ashi-waza), hip throws (Koshi-waza), and hand/shoulder throws (Te-waza) with mutual cooperation.
3. Katame-waza (Fundamental Holds): Learning core immobilization holds (Osaekomi-waza) with complete chest-to-chest chest control and safe release protocols.

To prioritize youth safety and healthy physical growth, all joint-locking techniques (Kansetsu-waza) and strangulation techniques (Shime-waza) are strictly excluded from the children's kata.

Structure

The Three Pedagogical Groups

The curriculum builds progressively from essential dojo manners and falling skills into dynamic standing throws and controlled ground holds.

GROUP 01 — FOUNDATIONS

Reihō, Shisei & Ukemi

礼法・姿勢・受身 — Etiquette & Falling

Teaching respect, spatial awareness, correct natural posture, sliding steps, and shock-absorbing breakfalls to prevent all injury.

  • 1. Ritsurei & Zarei Standing & Seated Bow
  • 2. Shizentai & Shintai Posture & Stepping
  • 3. Ushiro & Yoko Ukemi Rear & Side Breakfalls
  • 4. Mae-mawari Ukemi Forward Rolling Fall
GROUP 02 — THROWING

Kihon Nage-waza

基本投技 — Fundamental Throws

Learning the complete throwing cycle: breaking balance (Kuzushi), fitting the body (Tsukuri), and executing safe projections (Kake).

  • 5. Deashi-harai Advanced Foot Sweep
  • 6. O-goshi Major Hip Throw
  • 7. Ippon-seoi-nage One-Arm Shoulder Throw
  • 8. Osoto-gari Major Outer Reap
GROUP 03 — GROUNDWORK

Kihon Osaekomi-waza

基本抑込技 — Fundamental Holds

Immobilizing Uke supine on the tatami using solid hip base, chest-to-chest contact, and gentle, controlled weight distribution.

  • 9. Kesa-gatame Scarf Hold
  • 10. Yoko-shiho-gatame Side Four-Quarter Hold
  • 11. Kami-shiho-gatame Upper Four-Quarter Hold
  • 12. Kuzure-kesa-gatame Modified Scarf Hold

Step-by-Step

Technique Breakdown

The complete youth and junior syllabus detailed step-by-step according to the official Kodokan Judo textbook standard.

01
礼法 Ritsurei & Zarei "Standing Bow & Seated Bow"

The ceremonial foundation of Judo. Children learn proper standing bow (Ritsurei at 30 degrees) and formal kneeling bow (Zarei from Seiza), cultivating respect and safety toward partners and teachers.

1.Ritsurei: Stand in natural posture with heels together, incline the upper body smoothly forward 30 degrees, slide hands to top of knees, pause for 1 breath, and rise.
2.Zarei: Lower the left knee first, then right knee, sit back on heels in Seiza with big toes crossed, place hands flat in front forming a gentle triangle, incline the upper torso without lifting the hips, pause, and rise right knee first.
Kodokan Key Point: Reigi (Courtesy) — "Judo begins and ends with respect." A calm, respectful bow guarantees mutual safety during practice.
FoundationsRitsureiZareiDojo Etiquette
02
姿勢と進退 Shizentai & Shintai "Natural Posture & Footwork"

Mastering Shizen-hontai (natural standing posture with feet shoulder-width apart) and moving smoothly across the tatami using Tsugi-ashi (following footsteps) and Ayumi-ashi (natural walking footsteps) without crossing the feet.

1.Stand in Shizen-hontai: feet shoulder-width apart, knees soft, spine straight, shoulders relaxed, breathing centered in the abdomen.
2.Practice Suri-ashi (sliding steps) moving forward, backward, and sideways without dragging or lifting the feet high off the tatami.
Kodokan Key Point: Stability & balance — never cross the feet or lean the head forward while walking on the tatami.
FoundationsShizentaiSuri-ashiBalance Base
03
後ろ受身・横受身 Ushiro-ukemi & Yoko-ukemi "Rear Breakfall & Side Breakfall"

The essential protective skills for falling backward and sideways. Children tuck the chin tightly to protect the head, round the back like a ball, and strike the tatami with open palms at a 30–45 degree angle to absorb impact safely.

1.Ushiro-ukemi: Tuck the chin firmly to the chest, look at the belt/obi, roll backward smoothly like a ball, and slap the mat with both open palms at 35 degrees without letting the head touch the mat.
2.Yoko-ukemi: Step across, slide onto the side of the hip/ribs with chin tucked, slap the mat with the lead arm palm-down, and keep the legs separated to protect knees and ankles.
Kodokan Key Point: Head protection — the chin must remain tucked to the chest at all times so the back of the head never touches the tatami.
FoundationsUshiro-ukemiYoko-ukemiHead Safety
04
前回り受身 Mae-mawari Ukemi "Forward Rolling Breakfall (Zempo Kaiten)"

Rolling safely forward over the shoulder. Children form a rounded circle with the arms, tuck the head sideways, roll diagonally from fingertip across the shoulder to the opposite hip, and slap the mat cleanly to rise back onto the feet.

1.Step the right foot forward, place both hands on the mat forming a circular wheel, look past the left hip, and tuck the chin.
2.Push with the back leg, roll smoothly along the diagonal line from the right hand across the right shoulder blade to the left hip.
3.Slap the mat firmly with the left hand, extend the legs, and use the rolling momentum to stand smoothly back up in Shizentai.
Kodokan Key Point: Wheel curvature — maintain a round, curved arm and torso so the roll is smooth, silent, and free of bumping elbows or shoulders.
FoundationsForward RollUkemi MasteryRolling Recovery
05
出足払 Deashi-harai "Advanced Foot Sweep"

Tori draws Uke forward with standard collar-and-sleeve grip. At the exact instant Uke steps forward and begins transferring weight onto the front foot, Tori sweeps Uke's ankle across with the sole of the foot.

1.Tori retreats in natural walking steps, drawing Uke to take a step forward with the right foot.
2.Just before Uke plants full weight onto the right foot, Tori pulls the left sleeve and lifts the right collar to float Uke's balance.
3.Tori applies the curved sole of the left foot to the outside of Uke's right ankle, sweeping the foot cleanly across while supporting Uke with the sleeve.
Kodokan Key Point: Sweep timing & sleeve support — sweep like sweeping fallen leaves, and pull up firmly on the sleeve to ensure Uke lands safely in Yoko-ukemi.
Nage-wazaAshi-wazaFoot SweepTiming
06
大腰 O-goshi "Major Hip Throw"

Tori draws Uke forward onto the toes, wraps the right arm deeply around Uke's waist, lowers the hips below Uke's belt, and springs upward to roll Uke smoothly across the hips.

1.Tori steps forward-right, pulling Uke's left sleeve forward and upward to unbalance Uke onto the toes (Kuzushi).
2.Tori slides the right arm around Uke's waist, pivots 180 degrees, and places both feet between Uke's feet with knees bent low.
3.Tori straightens the knees, lifts Uke onto the hip fulcrum, bows forward, and rolls Uke cleanly over the hip.
Kodokan Key Point: Low hip fulcrum — bend the knees so Tori's belt line is lower than Uke's belt before lifting.
Nage-wazaKoshi-wazaHip ThrowWaist Grip
07
一本背負投 Ippon-seoi-nage "One-Arm Shoulder Throw"

Tori pulls Uke forward onto the toes, pivots 180 degrees, wedges the right arm deep into Uke's right armpit, clamps the upper arm, and throws Uke over the right shoulder.

1.Tori pulls Uke's right sleeve forward and upward with the left hand, drawing Uke onto the toes.
2.Tori releases the right collar, slides the right upper arm deep under Uke's right armpit, and pivots 180 degrees with knees bent.
3.Tori clamps Uke's arm tightly to the chest, extends the legs, bends forward, and projects Uke over the shoulder.
Kodokan Key Point: Armpit clamp & sleeve pull — clamp Uke's arm securely and maintain a strong two-handed control so Uke lands safely without head contact.
Nage-wazaTe-wazaShoulder ThrowArm Clamping
08
大外刈 Osoto-gari "Major Outer Reap"

Tori steps deep alongside Uke's right side, pulls Uke's weight completely onto the right heel, swings the right leg high forward, and reaps backward behind Uke's right knee.

1.Tori steps the left foot deep outside Uke's right foot, pulling the sleeve down and pushing the lapel back to fix Uke's weight onto the right heel.
2.Tori presses chest to chest, swings the right leg forward and upward like a pendulum.
3.Tori reaps the back of Uke's right knee with the back of the right thigh, driving forward to project Uke flat on the back.
Kodokan Key Point: Heel commitment — Uke's weight must be locked onto the heel before reaping; support Uke's sleeve so the head is protected on impact.
Nage-wazaAshi-wazaOuter ReapChest Contact
09
袈裟固 Kesa-gatame "Scarf Hold"

Tori sits beside Uke's right chest, wraps the left arm around Uke's neck to grip the back collar, clamps Uke's right arm tightly under the right armpit, and extends the legs in a wide, stable base.

1.Tori sits with the right hip touching Uke's right ribs, extending the right leg forward and bending the left leg back.
2.Tori wraps the left arm around Uke's neck to hold the deep collar, and clamps Uke's right upper arm securely under the right armpit.
3.Tori lowers the head, centers bodyweight down across Uke's chest, and maintains a stable base against escapes.
Kodokan Key Point: Wide leg base — keep hips firmly on the tatami with legs spread wide for stability; never lift the head up high.
Osaekomi-wazaScarf HoldChest PinGround Control
10
横四方固 Yoko-shiho-gatame "Side Four-Quarter Hold"

Tori controls Uke perpendicularly across the right flank, sliding the left arm under Uke's neck to grip the collar and the right arm between Uke's thighs to grip the rear belt.

1.Tori kneels perpendicularly beside Uke's right chest.
2.Tori slides the left arm under Uke's neck to hold the left collar, and threads the right arm between Uke's legs to grasp the back belt.
3.Tori lowers the chest flat across Uke's chest, spreading both knees wide with toes engaged in Kiza.
Kodokan Key Point: Perpendicular chest pressure — lower the chest directly onto Uke's center without leaning too far forward over the body.
Osaekomi-wazaSide HoldFour-Quarter PinBelt Grip
11
上四方固 Kami-shiho-gatame "Upper Four-Quarter Hold"

Tori approaches from directly above Uke's head, slides both arms under Uke's shoulders to grip both sides of the belt, and lowers the chest firmly onto Uke's chest.

1.Tori kneels at Uke's head in Kyoshi/Kiza posture.
2.Tori slides both arms underneath Uke's shoulders to firmly grasp both sides of Uke's belt.
3.Tori lowers the chest directly onto Uke's chest, rests the chin/forehead against Uke's lower abdomen, and spreads both knees wide.
Kodokan Key Point: Double underhook & knee spread — grip both sides of the belt firmly to prevent Uke from bridging or turning to either side.
Osaekomi-wazaUpper HoldHead-On PinBelt Grips
12
崩袈裟固 Kuzure-kesa-gatame "Modified Scarf Hold"

A variation of Kesa-gatame where Tori threads the left arm under Uke's right armpit (underhook) to hold the jacket collar, while clamping Uke's right arm under the right armpit.

1.Tori sits at Uke's right side in scarf hold posture with legs spread wide.
2.Instead of wrapping around the neck, Tori slides the left arm under Uke's right armpit to grip the back of Uke's collar or shoulder jacket.
3.Tori pulls Uke's shoulder close, clamps the right arm tightly, and presses the chest down firmly to conclude the kata.
Kodokan Key Point: Underhook control — underhooking the armpit gives Tori great mobility and prevents Uke from taking Tori's back.
Osaekomi-wazaModified ScarfUnderhook HoldKata Finale

Principles & Safety

Youth Reihō, Discipline & Safety Rules

Kodokan Judo for children is founded upon character development, mutual respect, and rigorous injury prevention.

Strict Age-Appropriate Safety

Children's judo strictly forbids all armlocks (Kansetsu-waza) and neck chokes (Shime-waza). Throwing techniques must always be accompanied by a secure sleeve grip to guide the partner safely to the mat.

Ukemi-First Methodology

A child must never be thrown before demonstrating confident, automatic breakfalls (Ukemi). When Ukemi is mastered, fear of falling disappears, allowing joyful technical growth.

Jita Kyoei (Mutual Prosperity)

Partners are not opponents to be hurt, but essential friends helping each other improve. Respecting one's partner with polite bows and gentle control embodies the true spirit of Kodokan Judo.
