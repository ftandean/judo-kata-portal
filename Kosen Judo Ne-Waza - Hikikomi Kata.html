Introduction

What is Kosen Judo Hikikomi & Ne-Waza?

Kosen Judo (高専柔道) was codified in the late 19th and early 20th centuries across the Japanese Higher Technical Schools (Koto Senmon Gakko) and preserved in the Seven Imperial Universities (Nanatei Judo). Under Kosen rules, competitors are permitted to pull their opponent directly into ground combat (Hikikomi) without attempting a sacrifice throw first.

This ruleset fostered an extraordinary evolution of ground fighting techniques (Ne-waza) that directly influenced modern Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu and submission grappling. The curriculum is built upon three continuous strategic phases:
1. Hikikomi-waza (Pull-in Entries 1–3): Direct guard pulls, hip-clamp entries, and sleeve-drag anchors that bring standing opponents down into controlled guard configurations.
2. Gaeshi & Torikaeshi (Sweeps & Turnovers 4–6): Classical bottom attacks including butterfly sweeps, Tomoe-roll transitions, and the iconic Sankaku (triangle) entrapments.
3. Fusegi-yaburi & Osae (Turtle Breakdown & Pins 7–9): Methodical methods to crack the defensive turtle (Kame), roll opponents into pins, and secure immediate strangles or locks.

Kosen doctrine prioritizes relentless pressure, hip connection, and positional patience, encapsulated in the motto "Never Stand Up Once Down."

Step-by-Step

Technique Breakdown

Filter by phase to analyze the grips, unbalancing leverage, and execution mechanics of Kosen groundwork.

01
胴締引込 Dō-jime Hikikomi "Direct Closed Guard Pull"

The foundational entry of Kosen Judo. Tori secures deep lapel and sleeve control, steps close between Uke's feet, jumps or drops the hips directly to the tatami, and crosses both ankles behind Uke's lower back into closed guard (Dō-osae).

1.Tori establishes a dominant collar grip with the right hand and controls Uke's right triceps sleeve with the left hand.
2.Tori steps the lead foot deep between Uke's legs, lowering the hips suddenly beneath Uke's center of gravity.
3.Tori pulls Uke's chest forward while locking both legs tightly around Uke's waist, closing the guard instantly.
Kosen Key Point: Break the standing posture simultaneously with the drop — never hang on Uke without pulling the collar down.

02
交差手引込 Kosa-dori Hikikomi "Cross-Sleeve Drag Guard Pull"

Tori grabs Uke's opposite sleeve in a cross-grip (Kosa-dori), drags Uke's arm across the centerline to expose the back flank, and drops the hips to slide under into an immediate back take or open guard entanglement.

1.Tori grips Uke's right sleeve with two hands (cross-grip) and steps to the left angle.
2.Tori snaps the arm across Uke's centerline (Arm Drag) to expose Uke's lat and shoulder.
3.Tori drops to the ground behind Uke's hip, inserting the far hook to initiate a back climb or side-guard control.
Kosen Key Point: Clear the arm across the body to eliminate Uke's posting hand before your hips touch the ground.
Pillar 1HikikomiArm DragBack Attack
03
足掛引込 Ashi-kake Hikikomi "Hip-Clamp / Open Guard Pull"

Tori places the sole of one foot directly on Uke's hip or inner thigh while dropping to the tatami, creating an unyielding distance frame and immediately establishing open guard (spider or De la Riva clamp).

1.Tori grips both of Uke's sleeves at the cuffs and steps back to create tension.
2.Tori places the right foot firmly on Uke's left hip crest and sits back with full control.
3.The second foot hooks behind Uke's opposite knee, controlling distance and neutralizing all standing throw attempts.
Kosen Key Point: Hip frame anchor — keep continuous pushing pressure on the hip to prevent Uke from blitzing past the guard on the drop.
Pillar 1HikikomiHip AnchorOpen Guard
04
隅返引込 Sumi-gaeshi Hikikomi "Butterfly / Corner Hook Reversal"

As Uke pressures forward into Tori's open guard, Tori inserts an instep hook under Uke's thigh (Butterfly hook), grips the back belt or overhook, falls backward, and elevates Uke directly overhead into mount.

1.From seated guard, Tori reaches deep over Uke's back to secure the belt while controlling the opposite triceps.
2.Tori inserts the right instep under Uke's left inner thigh and slides hips tight to Uke's base.
3.Tori rolls straight back, lifting with the instep hook to project Uke overhead and landing in Tate-shiho-gatame.
Kosen Key Point: Head in the armpit — bury your head tight to Uke's chest so your torso acts as a single fulcrum during the roll.
Pillar 2GaeshiButterfly SweepMount Turnover
05
三角絞返し Sankaku-jime Gaeshi "Bottom Triangle Turnover (Kosen Sankaku)"

The hallmark invention of Kosen Judo. When Uke attempts to pass with one arm inside the legs, Tori shoots the hips upward, locks the legs in a figure-four around Uke's neck and arm, and either finishes the strangle or rolls Uke over into a pin.

1.As Uke pushes one hand into Tori's guard, Tori pulls the trapped sleeve and shoots hips high into Uke's armpit.
2.Tori clamps the right knee over Uke's neck and locks the instep under the left knee (Sankaku figure-four).
3.Tori drags Uke's trapped arm across the neck, squeezes the knees for Sankaku-jime, or sweeps Uke over into Yoko-sankaku-gatame.
Kosen Key Point: Cut the 45-degree angle — never stay straight in front of Uke; turn perpendicular to secure a bone-deep knee lock.
Pillar 2SankakuTriangle ChokeSignature Kosen
06
巴返展開 Tomoe-gaeshi Tenkai "Circular Foot-in-Stomach Transition"

Tori feigns a classic Tomoe-nage sacrifice. When Uke braces low to resist the throw, Tori uses the foot on the lower abdomen as a pivot, spins beneath Uke's balance, and transitions to an unescapable Juji-gatame or back mount.

1.Tori places the foot on Uke's lower abdomen and drops back as if executing Tomoe-nage.
2.As Uke drops weight forward to block the throw, Tori pulls Uke's upper body sideways across the hips.
3.Tori swings the free leg over Uke's head, locking in a lightning-fast Ude-hishigi-juji-gatame (Armbar).
Kosen Key Point: Exploiting resistance — use Uke's downward resistance against the throw to funnel their arm directly into the armbar.
Pillar 2GaeshiTomoe TransitionJuji-gatame
07
妹尾返し Seno-gaeshi "Seno Roll (Rolling Turtle Turnover)"

Developed by Kosen legend Kyuzo Seno. When Uke forms a tight defensive turtle, Tori threads an arm through Uke's armpit to the opposite collar, front-somersaults over Uke's shoulder, and turns Uke directly into Kami-shiho-gatame.

1.Uke adopts a compact defensive turtle (Kame-fusegi). Tori positions alongside Uke's chest.
2.Tori weaves the near arm under Uke's armpit and grips the far collar while trapping Uke's far knee.
3.Tori dives forward into a front roll across Uke's back, peeling Uke onto the shoulders into Kuzure-kami-shiho-gatame.
Kosen Key Point: Deep collar feed — secure the collar grip deep beneath the throat before rolling to lock Uke's head to your chest.
Pillar 3Turtle CrackSeno-gaeshiRolling Turnover
08
片手絞崩 Kata-te-jime Kuzushi "One-Hand Lapel Strangle & Clock Turnover"

Tori attacks a turtled Uke by inserting a deep thumb-in collar grip under the chin, stepping the legs around Uke's head in a clock-like rotation, and driving bodyweight down to force a tap or roll.

1.Tori inserts the right hand deep into Uke's right lapel with four fingers outside and thumb inside.
2.Tori drops the right shoulder across Uke's cervical spine and controls Uke's far wrist or pants.
3.Tori walks the legs forward in a circular clock motion, strangling Uke or rolling them over into Osaekomi.
Kosen Key Point: Heavy shoulder pressure — never strangle with arms alone; drop the entirety of your chest weight onto the back of Uke's neck.
Pillar 3Turtle CrackClock ChokeLapel Strangle
09
横三角固 Yoko-sankaku-gatame "Side Triangle Turtle Pin"

Tori attacks Uke's turtle from the side, shooting the legs between Uke's elbow and knee to lock a side triangle around the neck and shoulder, rolling Uke over into a completely inescapable pin.

1.Tori inserts the top knee through the space between Uke's triceps and thigh from the flank.
2.Tori locks the figure-four Sankaku across Uke's neck and trapped arm from the top side.
3.Tori rolls Uke over onto their back, holding Uke's legs and controlling the upper torso for an ippon pin.
Kosen Key Point: Hip wedge penetration — slide the knee through the exact moment Uke shifts weight or lifts an elbow.

Principles & Doctrine

The Strategic Tenets of Kosen Groundwork

The tactical philosophy that governed inter-university rivalry and forged legendary groundwork endurance.

"Never Stand Up Once Down"

Kosen doctrine dictates that once combat transitions to the mat, the player must commit 100% to progressing through guard or turtle breakdowns until a submission or pin is achieved.

The Invention of Sankaku

The entire family of triangle locks (Sankaku-jime, Yoko-sankaku, Ushiro-sankaku) was invented and perfected by Kosen judoka (notably Tsunetane Oda and his peers) in the 1910s and 1920s.

Hikiwake Strategy & Team Depth

In Nanatei team tournaments, securing a draw (Hikiwake) from the guard against a stronger thrower is a vital team victory, eliminating dangerous aces through relentless ne-waza stalemate.
