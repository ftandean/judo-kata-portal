Introduction

What is Keisatsu Taiho-jutsu?

Keisatsu Taiho-jutsu (警察逮捕術), meaning "Police Arresting Art," is the standardized defensive tactics and subject-control martial art developed for law enforcement officers in Japan following World War II.

Formulated by a joint council of masters from Kodokan Judo, Kendo, Aikido, Nihon Jujutsu, and Karate-do, the system replaced feudal-era apprehension methods with an evidence-based, legally proportionate doctrine.

Unlike sport martial arts where maximum impact or scoring an Ippon is the objective, the guiding philosophy of Taiho-jutsu is Safety, Restraint, and Proportionality. Officers are trained to neutralize aggression with minimal injury, safely disarm weapons, and transition smoothly into prone pinning and handcuffing (Tejō-kake).

The kata incorporates empty-hand self-defense (Toshu-waza), edged weapon disarming (Tanto-waza), and telescopic baton control (Keibo-waza), serving as the technical core of police academy defensive training across Japan.

Structure

The Three Sets

Taiho-jutsu Kata is structured into three distinct domains of tactical engagement, progressing from empty-hand restraint to armed disarms and baton leverage.

SET 01

Toshu Taiho-waza

徒手逮捕技 — Empty-Hand Restraint

Neutralizing unarmed grabs, punches, and rear bear-hugs using balance breaks, joint locks, takedowns, and immediate prone pinning.

  • 1. Katate-dori Kote-gaeshi Wrist Twist
  • 2. Ryōte-dori Ude-gatame Straight Arm Lock
  • 3. Mune-dori Hiki-taoshi Lapel Pull-Down
  • 4. Tsuki-komi Waki-gatame Armpit Entanglement
  • 5. Ushiro-dori Osae Rear Hug Escape
SET 02

Tanto Taiho-waza

短刀逮捕技 — Knife Disarming Forms

Defensive body shifting (Tai-sabaki), blade redirection, joint lock disarms, and ground control against straight, diagonal, and overhead knife attacks.

  • 6. Choku-tsuki Kote-osae Thrust Arm Lock
  • 7. Naname-tsuki Shihō-nage Diagonal Throw
  • 8. Kiri-oroshi Kote-gaeshi Downward Cut Reversal
  • 9. Hiki-bari Ude-garami Slash Entanglement
  • 10. Tsuki-age Soto-waki-gatame Rising Thrust Lock
SET 03

Keibō Taiho-waza

警棒逮捕技 — Police Baton Tactics

Deploying the telescopic baton for reinforced blocks, controlled strikes to motor points, mechanical lever locks, and final handcuffing integration.

  • 11. Men-uchi Uke-dome High Baton Block
  • 12. Dō-uchi Tsuki-kaeshi Flank Thrust Counter
  • 13. Keibō Kansetsu-osae Baton Lever Lock
  • 14. Keibō Kubi-garami Shoulder/Neck Frame
  • 15. Sōgō Osaekomi-Tejō Pin & Handcuff

Step-by-Step

Technique Breakdown

Detailed breakdown of subject attack momentum, officer evasion/interception, joint control mechanics, and transition into custody.

01
片手取小手返 Katate-dori Kote-gaeshi "Single Wrist Seize countered by Wrist Outward Twist"

The subject aggressively grabs the officer's right wrist. The officer applies circular Tai-sabaki, clamps the subject's seizing hand, twists the wrist outwardly, and drives the subject prone for control.

1.Subject advances and firmly grasps Officer's right wrist with their left hand.
2.Officer steps diagonally back-right, rotating the captured arm upward in a circular release arc.
3.Officer covers the back of Subject's grasping hand with the left palm, thumb applying pressure between the 4th and 5th metacarpals.
4.Officer torques the wrist in an outward spiral (Kote-gaeshi), bringing Subject face-down into a prone immobilization.
Tactical Key Point: Continuous wrist hyperflexion — Officer maintains downward pressure on the joint after the takedown to prevent rolling while issuing clear verbal commands.
Toshu-wazaWrist ReleaseKote-gaeshi
02
両手取腕固 Ryōte-dori Ude-gatame "Both Wrists Seized countered by Straight Arm Lock"

The subject grips both of the officer's wrists to impede movement. The officer spirals one arm free, steps inside, applies straight arm pressure across the subject's extended elbow, and secures a takedown.

1.Subject steps in aggressively, clutching both of Officer's wrists.
2.Officer rotates the right forearm inward in an outward spiral, peeling Subject's left thumb grip.
3.Officer clamps Subject's right wrist with the left hand and places both hands across the hyperextended elbow joint.
4.Officer drops the center of mass, directing downward force through the elbow axis (Ude-gatame) to force Subject prone.
Tactical Key Point: Hip-sinking leverage — the lock is executed by lowering the center of gravity rather than relying on shoulder muscular force.
Toshu-wazaJoint RestraintUde-gatame
03
胸取引倒 Mune-dori Hiki-taoshi "Lapel Grab countered by Pull-Down Control"

The subject clenches the officer's uniform lapel or chest aggressively. The officer traps the grabbing hand, executes an off-balancing rear pivot, peels the wrist, and forces a chest-down grounding.

1.Subject advances and grabs Officer's uniform chest/lapel with the right hand to shove or pull.
2.Officer clamps Subject's grabbing hand firmly against the chest with the left hand to deny release.
3.Officer takes a deep rearward sliding step (Tai-sabaki) while applying downward rotational torque on the wrist.
4.Subject's posture breaks forward-diagonal, driving them directly into a controlled prone landing.
Tactical Key Point: Pinning before pivoting — firmly trapping the hand against the torso turns the officer's body rotation into an unescapable wrist lock.
Toshu-wazaLapel DefenseHiki-taoshi
04
突込脇固 Tsuki-komi Waki-gatame "Straight Punch countered by Armpit Entanglement"

The subject throws a straight punch to the officer's face. The officer slips outside the strike, entraps the extended arm beneath the armpit (Waki-gatame), and forces a rapid prone submission.

1.Subject lunges forward with a committed right straight punch aimed at Officer's head.
2.Officer executes a rapid lateral step off the centerline, guiding Subject's punching wrist with the left hand.
3.Officer traps Subject's outstretched arm under the right armpit, locking the elbow joint facing upward.
4.Officer rotates downward, driving Subject flat to the mat with immediate escort lock control.
Tactical Key Point: Controlled pressure — Waki-gatame must be applied smoothly to induce rapid submission without causing accidental compound joint rupture.
Toshu-wazaStrike InterceptionWaki-gatame
05
後取抑 Ushiro-dori Osae "Rear Bear-Hug countered by Stance Drop and Pin"

The subject ambushes the officer from behind with a bear-hug around the arms. The officer drops into a low base (Jigotai), breaks the grip, turns inside the hold, and grounds the subject into a shoulder pin.

1.Subject locks both arms tightly around Officer's torso and arms from behind.
2.Officer immediately sinks the hips in Jigotai and raises both elbows outward to loosen the clasp.
3.Officer stomps the instep, pivots 180 degrees inside the grip, and traps Subject's right wrist.
4.Officer guides Subject forward-downward into a definitive ground shoulder lock.
Tactical Key Point: Instant pelvic drop — lowering the center of mass prevents the subject from lifting or slamming the officer backward.
Toshu-wazaRear AmbushGrip Break
06
直突小手押 Choku-tsuki Kote-osae "Straight Knife Thrust countered by Forearm Press Lock"

The subject lunges forward with a straight knife thrust to the midsection. The officer parries to the outside flank, establishes two-on-one wrist control, forces an elbow lock (Kote-osae / Ikkyo), and strips the weapon.

1.Subject drives a direct underhand knife thrust (Choku-tsuki) toward Officer's abdomen.
2.Officer steps diagonally forward-left (Tai-sabaki), clearing the blade path while checking Subject's wrist.
3.Officer secures Subject's armed wrist with both hands, driving downward leverage across the forearm and shoulder.
4.Subject is grounded prone; Officer places a knee pin on the tricep and peels the knife away cleanly.
Tactical Key Point: Weapon isolation — never attempt to strip the knife until Subject's shoulder and elbow joints are completely stabilized against the mat.
Tanto-wazaBlade DefenseKote-osae
07
斜突四方投 Naname-tsuki Shihō-nage "Diagonal Knife Thrust countered by Four-Direction Throw"

The subject lunges with a diagonal overhand knife stab. The officer steps under the attack vector, captures the knife wrist, rotates 180 degrees beneath the arm, and casts the subject down with Shihō-nage.

1.Subject attacks with an overhand diagonal stab (Naname-tsuki) aimed at Officer's neck/collarbone.
2.Officer enters under the weapon arm, intercepting the armed wrist with both hands.
3.Officer executes a 180-degree pivot (Tenkan) under Subject's arm, bending Subject's wrist back toward their own shoulder.
4.Officer cuts downward, casting Subject backwards flat onto the mat and peeling the weapon on impact.
Tactical Key Point: Clearing the blade radius — the body turn must position Officer completely outside the blade's sweep before beginning the downward cut.
Tanto-wazaDiagonal StabShihō-nage
08
切下小手返 Kiri-oroshi Kote-gaeshi "Downward Knife Slash countered by Outward Wrist Reversal"

The subject brings the knife straight down overhead in a violent slashing motion. The officer parries the wrist early, steps to the flank, torques the wrist in Kote-gaeshi, and disarms on the ground.

1.Subject raises the knife overhead and strikes downward toward Officer's head.
2.Officer steps off-line to the flank and parries the descending wrist before maximum velocity is reached.
3.Officer clamps both hands on Subject's right hand, applying an outward circular twist to the wrist.
4.Subject is flipped down; Officer immediately rolls them prone, secures the wrist, and recovers the weapon.
Tactical Key Point: Early wrist interception — checking high on the descending arm prevents the blade from reaching dangerous momentum at chest level.
Tanto-wazaOverhead SlashKote-gaeshi
09
引払腕絡 Hiki-bari Ude-garami "Horizontal Knife Slash countered by Entangled Arm Lock"

The subject swings a wide horizontal backhand knife slash. The officer ducks beneath the blade arc, weaves the weapon arm into a figure-four lock (Ude-garami), and forces a rapid submission.

1.Subject swings a wide horizontal backhand slash aimed at Officer's torso/throat.
2.Officer steps inside the sweep with a lowered center of mass, ducking beneath the cutting radius.
3.Officer threads the right arm over Subject's elbow and clasps the left wrist in a figure-four (Ude-garami).
4.Officer applies rotational pressure to the shoulder, compelling Subject to drop the blade and submit flat on the mat.
Tactical Key Point: Inside entry timing — moving inside the arc deprives the knife of lever power and immediately isolates the arm.
Tanto-wazaHorizontal SlashUde-garami
10
突上外脇固 Tsuki-age Soto-waki-gatame "Rising Uppercut Thrust countered by Outer Armpit Lock"

The subject lunges upward with a concealed uppercut knife thrust. The officer sweeps the blade across the centerline, traps the elbow against the ribs, and drives the subject prone with Soto-waki-gatame.

1.Subject drives a concealed upward stab (Tsuki-age) aimed at Officer's midsection.
2.Officer shifts the hips back, using a sweeping downward palm parry to redirect the thrust across the body.
3.Officer locks Subject's extended right wrist with both hands, clamping Subject's elbow against Officer's torso.
4.Officer applies downward rotational leverage, breaking Subject's base and pinning them prone for weapon recovery.
Tactical Key Point: Centerline deflection — guiding the thrust across the torso ensures that even under maximum pressure, the blade points away from vital organs.
Tanto-wazaUppercut ThrustOuter Arm Lock
11
面打受止 Men-uchi Uke-dome "Overhead Club Strike countered by Two-Hand Baton High Block"

An armed subject swings a club overhead. The officer extends the Keibō in a reinforced two-handed high block, deflects the strike at a 45-degree angle, steps in, and executes a controlled takedown.

1.Subject raises a blunt weapon (pipe/club) and executes an overhead strike toward Officer's head.
2.Officer holds the Keibō horizontally with both hands (Jōdan Uke) and intercepts the blow at a 45-degree deflection angle.
3.Officer drives forward inside Subject's reach, checking Subject's striking arm.
4.Officer uses the baton tip as a frame against Subject's clavicle/shoulder, forcing a backward takedown.
Tactical Key Point: Angled deflection — holding the baton at a 45-degree angle prevents impact shock transfer to the wrists and redirects the weapon cleanly.
Keibō-wazaBaton BlockBlunt Weapon Defense
12
胴打突返 Dō-uchi Tsuki-kaeshi "Midsection Strike countered by Baton Thrust Counter"

The subject swings a lateral strike at the officer's ribs. The officer retreats slightly, blocks downward with the baton shaft, and executes a sharp stop-thrust into the subject's torso to halt aggression.

1.Subject swings a wide horizontal blow aimed at Officer's midsection/ribs.
2.Officer slides the lead leg back in Tsugi-ashi, using the vertical Keibō shaft to absorb the impact.
3.Officer fires an immediate linear stop-thrust (Tsuki) with the baton tip into Subject's abdominal wall.
4.Subject stumbles back off-balance, breaking attack momentum and enabling officer containment.
Tactical Key Point: Target regulation — strikes are strictly restricted to large muscle groups (abdomen, thighs, arms), avoiding the head, neck, and spine.
Keibō-wazaLateral BlockStop Thrust
13
警棒関節抑 Keibō Kansetsu-osae "Baton-Assisted Joint Lever Pin"

The subject stiffens their arms and resists standing escort. The officer threads the baton behind the subject's elbow crook, creates a mechanical lever, and forces the subject prone without injury.

1.Subject locks their arms and violently resists standard empty-hand escort control.
2.Officer inserts the extended Keibō shaft behind Subject's elbow crook and over the forearm.
3.Officer levers the baton shaft downward while lifting Subject's wrist, applying a smooth fulcrum lock.
4.Subject is guided smoothly to the mat into a locked, non-injurious prone pinning position.
Tactical Key Point: Mechanical advantage — using the baton as a class-two lever enables smaller officers to control larger resisting subjects safely.
Keibō-wazaLever LockEscort Control
14
警棒首絡 Keibō Kubi-garami "Baton Shoulder/Neck Guiding Control"

A charging subject attempts a tackle. The officer pivots off the assault line, places the padded baton shaft across the subject's shoulder girdle, and redirects momentum downward onto the mat.

1.Subject charges forward attempting to tackle or wrap Officer around the waist.
2.Officer executes a sharp evasive pivot (Tai-sabaki), allowing Subject's charge to overshoot.
3.Officer places the central section of the Keibō across Subject's shoulder and upper chest girdle.
4.Officer guides Subject in a downward circular arc directly onto the floor.
Tactical Key Point: No airway restriction — the baton acts as a structural frame against the shoulder and upper chest, strictly avoiding trachea compression.
Keibō-wazaTackle DefenseMomentum Redirection
15
総合抑込手錠 Sōgō Osaekomi-Tejō "Comprehensive Prone Pin and Handcuff Application"

The culminating arrest sequence. Grounding the subject, securing a three-point knee pin across the shoulder and hip, drawing the handcuffs (Tejō), and applying double-locked cuffs safely.

1.Officer brings Subject fully prone (face-down) with one arm secured behind the back in an arrest lock.
2.Officer places the right knee lightly behind Subject's shoulder blade and the left knee stabilizing the hip base.
3.Officer maintains verbal commands, draws standard police handcuffs (Tejō) with the free hand.
4.Officer applies the cuffs bottom-up on both wrists, verifies double-lock engagement, and conducts a safety search.
Tactical Key Point: Positional safety — once handcuffed, the subject is immediately rolled into a recovery position or seated upright to ensure unrestricted breathing.

Principles & Legal Doctrine

Core Pillars of Police Taiho-jutsu

Japanese Police defensive tactics operate strictly within national legal standards governing proportional use of force.

Proportionality (適合性)

Force must directly correspond to the threat level presented by the suspect. Officers transition dynamically between verbal commands, empty-hand control, baton leverage, and firearm readiness as situations evolve.

Minimum Harm (最小限の危害)

Techniques are chosen to subdue and disarm without causing permanent orthopedic or neurological injury. Joint locks emphasize compliance pain and balance breaks rather than joint destruction.

Tejō-kake & Restraint (手錠施錠)

Every defensive sequence culminates in safe, procedural handcuffing. Officers are trained to continuously monitor suspect consciousness, breathing, and circulation immediately following prone restraint.
