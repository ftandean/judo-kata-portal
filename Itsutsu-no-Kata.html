Introduction

What is Itsutsu-no-Kata?

Itsutsu-no-Kata (五の形), meaning the "Forms of Five," is the most abstract, philosophical, and aesthetically elevated kata in Kodokan Judo. Formulated by Jigoro Kano around 1887, it draws deeply from the cosmology and biomechanics of Tenjin Shinyo-ryu and Kito-ryu Jujutsu.

Unlike other kata, the five techniques of Itsutsu-no-Kata were intentionally left unnamed by Jigoro Kano. They are formally referred to simply as Dai-ikkyo (First Form) through Dai-gokyo (Fifth Form) to avoid confining their vast universal principles to single technical labels.

The kata demonstrates the laws of physics that govern Judo and the natural universe: the direct application of unceasing energy, yielding redirection to evade an overwhelming charge, the centrifugal whirlpool of water, the irresistible rhythm of ocean tides, and the complete yielding void that neutralizes a head-on collision.

Execution requires sublime harmony, seamless breathing (Kokyu), unified center of gravity (Tanden), and perfect mutual synchronization between Tori and Uke.

Structure

The Five Universal Principles

Each form illustrates a fundamental law of force, momentum, and cosmic balance through paired physical interaction.

FORM 01

Dai-ikkyo

第一教 — Continuous Positive Energy

Illustrates the principle that a small, continuous, micro-adjusting force applied persistently can overcome a massive, stationary opposing body.

FORM 02

Dai-nikyo

第二教 — Yielding Redirection

Illustrates the core principle of Ju: yielding to an overwhelming rushing attack, redirecting its vector, and allowing its momentum to cause self-defeat.

FORM 03

Dai-sankyo

第三教 — The Whirlpool (Centrifugal Force)

Demonstrates the vortex or whirlpool effect: spinning around an immovable vertical center of rotation to control and throw the adversary outward.

FORM 04

Dai-yonkyo

第四教 — The Ocean Tide (Ebb and Flow)

Illustrates the immense, rhythmic ebb and flow of ocean tides: drawing in the opponent smoothly before sweeping forward in an irresistible wave.

FORM 05

Dai-gokyo

第五教 — The Yielding Void (Cosmic Harmony)

Illustrates the universe absorbing a headlong collision: yielding entirely into a void, preserving mutual safety, and floating together in cosmic harmony.

Step-by-Step

Technique Breakdown

The five forms detailed step-by-step according to the official Kodokan Judo textbook standard.

01
第一教 Dai-ikkyo "The First Form — Continuous Direct Energy"

Tori approaches Uke in natural posture, places the right palm lightly on Uke's chest, and applies steady, continuous, focused forward pressure (Oshi) without sudden shoving, driving Uke backward step by step until Uke loses balance and falls rearward.

1.Tori and Uke stand facing each other in Shizen-hontai at standard distance (approx. 1.8m).
2.Tori steps forward with the right foot, placing the right palm flat against Uke's sternum/chest, keeping the left hand natural.
3.Tori applies continuous, micro-adjusting forward driving force from the center of gravity (Tanden) in Tsugi-ashi.
4.Uke is forced backward step by step, gradually overbalanced onto the heels, and collapses straight backward to the mat.
Kodokan Key Point: Unbroken pressure — no sudden muscular thrust or pushing impact. The energy must flow in an unbroken, constant stream from Tori's core into Uke's spine.
Principle 1Continuous ForceTanden DriveLinear Kuzushi
02
第二教 Dai-nikyo "The Second Form — Yielding Redirection"

Uke charges forward with aggressive, powerful pushing force. Tori does not resist or brace, but steps back smoothly in a circular arc, leading Uke's force past the body and using Uke's own momentum to project Uke forward.

1.Uke advances aggressively toward Tori with outstretched arms and strong forward momentum.
2.Tori meets Uke's oncoming hands lightly, yielding immediately by stepping backward in a sweeping circular Tai-sabaki.
3.Tori guides Uke's arms along their natural vector of attack, accelerating Uke's forward momentum.
4.Uke overextends completely into empty space, pitches forward onto the toes, and falls cleanly to the tatami.
Kodokan Key Point: The core of Ju (Yielding) — meeting strong force with zero resistance, adding guidance to the trajectory, and letting the attacker overthrow themselves.
Principle 2Yielding to ForceCircular EvasionSelf-Overthrow
03
第三教 Dai-sankyo "The Third Form — The Whirlpool (Centrifugal Force)"

Tori and Uke connect in the center, and Tori initiates a powerful spinning vortex. Tori acts as the calm, immovable central axis of a whirlpool while drawing Uke into the outer spiral, launching Uke outward by centrifugal force.

1.Tori and Uke step into close contact, engaging hand contact in the center.
2.Tori establishes an erect, immovable vertical axis of rotation, spinning rapidly on the spot in a tight circle.
3.Uke is drawn into the outer orbit of the vortex, revolving at high speed around Tori's core.
4.Tori releases/extends the centrifugal arc, casting Uke outward in a wide, sweeping fall to the mat.
Kodokan Key Point: The water vortex — Tori must remain perfectly upright and centered as the stationary core, while Uke experiences maximum centrifugal velocity at the perimeter.
Principle 3Vortex / WhirlpoolCentrifugal ForceRotational Axis
04
第四教 Dai-yonkyo "The Fourth Form — The Ocean Tide (Ebb and Flow)"

Tori draws Uke forward in a retreating, receding motion like the ebbing tide drawing water out to sea, and then immediately surges forward like a surging tsunami wave, projecting Uke in an irresistible tide.

1.Both engage at close range with palms in contact.
2.Tori glides smoothly backward in a long, rhythmic retreat, drawing Uke forward into deep overextension (The Ebbing Tide — Hiki-shio).
3.Without a pause, Tori reverses direction, swelling forward with surging, whole-body power (The Surging Tide — Michi-shio).
4.Tori sweeps through Uke's center, throwing Uke backward onto the tatami like an ocean wave breaking on the shore.
Kodokan Key Point: Tidal rhythm — the transition from receding retreat (ebb) to surging projection (flow) must be continuous, seamless, and rhythmic without stopping.
Principle 4Ocean TideEbb & FlowRhythmic Surge
05
第五教 Dai-gokyo "The Fifth Form — The Yielding Void (Cosmic Harmony)"

The fifth and supreme form. Tori and Uke run toward each other from opposite sides of the mat at full speed. At the instant of collision, Tori yields completely, drops beneath Uke into a circular void, and both float together in safe, harmonized cosmic orbit.

1.Tori and Uke start from opposite ends of the tatami (approx. 5.5 meters apart).
2.Both run directly toward each other at high velocity, appearing destined for a catastrophic head-on impact.
3.At the exact moment of contact, Tori creates an absolute void by dropping the hips and yielding underneath Uke's charge (classical Tomoe-nage / floating sacrifice).
4.Uke floats in a grand overhead celestial arc; both land and rise in harmonious mutual balance, concluding the kata.
Kodokan Key Point: Mutual welfare (Jita Kyoei) — converting a destructive head-on collision into an elevated, circular, harmonious orbit where both partners preserve mutual safety and balance.

Philosophy & Etiquette

Kodokan Reihō & Cosmic Principles

Itsutsu-no-Kata represents the theoretical foundation of Jigoro Kano's philosophical doctrine.

Unnamed Tradition (Mujingyō)

Kano deliberately did not name the five techniques. By leaving them numbered (Dai-ikkyo to Dai-gokyo), he ensured that practitioners would not reduce these universal physical principles to static mechanical forms.

Seiryoku Zen'yo & Jita Kyoei

The kata demonstrates Maximum Efficiency with Minimum Effort (Seiryoku Zen'yo) and Mutual Welfare and Benefit (Jita Kyoei) through the laws of momentum, gravity, centrifugal energy, and fluid dynamics.

Reihō & Spatial Protocol

Tori and Uke stand 5.5 meters apart. They perform standing bow (Ritsurei) to Joseki, face each other, drop into Seiza for seated bow (Zarei), rise, and step forward into Shizen-hontai to begin Dai-ikkyo.
