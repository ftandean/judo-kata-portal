Introduction

What is Nage-no-Kata?

Nage-no-Kata (投の形) is the throwing counterpart to Katame-no-Kata within Kodokan Judo's Randori-no-Kata. Formalized between 1884 and 1887 under the direct guidance of Jigoro Kano, it serves as the ultimate pedagogical study of throwing mechanics.

The kata is composed of 15 representative throwing techniques organized into five distinct classifications of three techniques each: hand techniques (Te-waza), hip techniques (Koshi-waza), foot sweeps and reaps (Ashi-waza), rear sacrifice throws (Ma-sutemi-waza), and side sacrifice throws (Yoko-sutemi-waza).

A mandatory Kodokan standard of Nage-no-Kata is that every technique is performed twice: first on the right side (Migi-waza) and then immediately on the left side (Hidari-waza), requiring a total of 30 dynamic throws. Both partners must maintain fluid walking rhythm (Tsugi-ashi and Ayumi-ashi) and flawless posture (Shizentai).

Execution requires pure mastery over the three phases of throwing: breaking the opponent's balance (Kuzushi), fitting the body efficiently into the attack vector (Tsukuri), and completing the projection with full control (Kake).

Structure

The Five Sets (Throwing Classifications)

Nage-no-Kata is categorized into five distinct technical sets based on the primary bodily mechanism and trajectory of the throw.

SET 01

Te-waza

手技 — Hand & Shoulder Techniques

Throws initiated primarily through upper body off-balancing, pulling vectors, and shoulder leverage.

  • 1. Uki-otoshi Floating Drop
  • 2. Seoi-nage Shoulder Throw
  • 3. Kata-guruma Shoulder Wheel
SET 02

Koshi-waza

腰技 — Hip Techniques

Loading and projecting Uke across Tori's pelvic fulcrum using rotational hip contact and elevation.

  • 4. Uki-goshi Floating Hip
  • 5. Harai-goshi Sweeping Hip
  • 6. Tsurikomi-goshi Lifting-Pulling Hip
SET 03

Ashi-waza

足技 — Foot & Leg Techniques

Disrupting Uke's base at the moment weight transfers onto or off the supporting foot.

  • 7. Okuri-ashi-harai Foot Sweep
  • 8. Sasae-tsurikomi-ashi Propping Ankle
  • 9. Uchi-mata Inner Thigh Throw
SET 04

Ma-sutemi-waza

真捨身技 — Rear Sacrifice Techniques

Tori intentionally sacrifices standing posture directly backward to mat to propel Uke overhead.

  • 10. Tomoe-nage Circular Stomach Throw
  • 11. Ura-nage Rear Throw
  • 12. Sumi-gaeshi Corner Reversal
SET 05

Yoko-sutemi-waza

橫捨身技 — Side Sacrifice Techniques

Tori sacrifices balance onto the side flank to whip Uke in a large lateral circular trajectory.

  • 13. Yoko-gake Side Hook
  • 14. Yoko-guruma Side Wheel
  • 15. Uki-waza Floating Technique

Step-by-Step

Technique Breakdown

Each technique is detailed according to the official Kodokan Judo textbook standard (performed Right then Left).

01
浮落 Uki-otoshi "Floating Drop"

The opening technique of Nage-no-Kata. Tori steps backward in Tsugi-ashi while Uke advances with pushing pressure. Tori drops onto the left knee without body contact, pulling Uke down in a circular arc.

1.Both take right natural grip (Migi-shizentai). Uke advances three steps with pushing pressure; Tori retreats three steps in Tsugi-ashi.
2.On the third step, Tori pulls strongly with both hands to draw Uke's weight forward onto the right toes (Kuzushi).
3.Tori drops the left knee to the tatami, pulling both hands downward and backward in a large sweeping circle to project Uke forward (Kake).
Kodokan Key Point: No body contact — the throw relies purely on hand pull, body drop, and timing Uke's forward momentum. Repeated immediately on the left side (Hidari).
Te-wazaKnee DropPure Hand Throw
02
背負投 Seoi-nage "Shoulder Throw"

Uke steps forward delivering a downward hammer-fist blow (Tegatana) toward Tori's crown. Tori evades underneath the strike, clamps Uke's right arm, and projects Uke over the back.

1.Uke steps forward with the right foot, raising the right fist to strike down at the center of Tori's forehead.
2.Tori steps forward-left inside Uke's reach, pivots 180 degrees, and wedges the right upper arm deep into Uke's right armpit while pulling Uke's right sleeve.
3.Tori lowers the hips, extends the legs, bends forward at the waist, and throws Uke cleanly over the shoulder.
Kodokan Key Point: Sen-no-sen entry — entering beneath the downward strike before it finishes descending. Repeated on the left side.
Te-wazaStrike DefenseShoulder Pivot
03
肩車 Kata-guruma "Shoulder Wheel"

Tori retreats three steps, pulls Uke forward into deep Kuzushi, ducks deep between Uke's legs to drape Uke over both shoulders, and wheels Uke onto the mat.

1.Tori and Uke engage in right grip; Uke pushes forward for three steps while Tori retreats.
2.On the third step, Tori breaks Uke's balance forward-right, sinks hips deeply, and slides the right arm between Uke's thighs while pulling the right sleeve across the neck.
3.Tori stands upright lifting Uke across both shoulders, then turns and drops forward-left to wheel Uke to the tatami.
Kodokan Key Point: Deep knee bend — Tori's center of gravity must sink lower than Uke's hips before hoisting. Repeated on the left side.
Te-wazaShoulder HoistDeep Lowering
04
浮腰 Uki-goshi "Floating Hip"

The definitive hip throw of Kodokan Judo. Uke steps in to strike Tori's head. Tori ducks inside, wraps the right arm around Uke's waist, and rotates the hips half-way to float Uke off the tatami.

1.Uke steps forward with the right foot to strike Tori's forehead with a downward chopping fist.
2.Tori steps forward-left inside the blow, slides the right arm tightly around Uke's waist, and fits the right hip against Uke's abdomen (half-hip turn).
3.Tori twists the upper torso and hips swiftly without bending over, projecting Uke in a lateral spinning arc.
Kodokan Key Point: Half-hip rotation — unlike O-goshi, Tori uses a shallow hip placement and rapid torsional spin. Repeated on the left side.
Koshi-wazaShallow HipTorsional Twist
05
払腰 Harai-goshi "Sweeping Hip Throw"

Moving in circular walking steps, Tori draws Uke forward, wraps the back collar, inserts the hip, and sweeps Uke's right outer thigh with the right leg.

1.Tori and Uke step in a wide circular pattern; Tori pulls Uke forward-right onto the toes.
2.Tori reaches the right hand over Uke's left shoulder to grip the back collar, planting the left foot close to Uke's center.
3.Tori sweeps the right leg backward along the outer line of Uke's right thigh while rotating the chest to complete the throw.
Kodokan Key Point: Thigh sweep coordination — the leg sweep and chest rotation must happen simultaneously as Uke rises onto the toes. Repeated on the left side.
Koshi-wazaLeg SweepCircular Movement
06
釣込腰 Tsurikomi-goshi "Lifting-Pulling Hip Throw"

Uke assumes a rigid defensive posture (Jigotai) pushing forward. Tori yields, lowers the hips deep beneath Uke's belt line, and lifts while pulling the lapel high to project Uke forward.

1.Both assume defensive posture (Jigotai); Uke pushes Tori backward with stiff arms.
2.Tori yields on the third step, lifts upward with the right lapel grip (Tsurite), and pulls forward-down with the left sleeve (Hikite).
3.Tori pivots 180 degrees, drops hips below Uke's center of mass, straightens the legs, and springs Uke over the hips.
Kodokan Key Point: High Tsurite lift — the right wrist pushes upward like an umbrella handle to break Jigotai. Repeated on the left side.
Koshi-wazaJigotai CounterLifting Sleeve/Lapel
07
送足払 Okuri-ashi-harai "Foot Sweep / Sliding Foot Sweep"

Both partners move sideways across the tatami in fast Tsugi-ashi. Tori sweeps Uke's trailing ankle into the leading ankle just as Uke brings both feet together.

1.Tori and Uke grip in Shizentai and travel laterally across the mat in rapid sideways sliding steps (Tsugi-ashi).
2.On the third lateral step, Tori lifts Uke slightly with the Tsurite, causing Uke to float momentarily.
3.Tori applies the sole of the left foot to Uke's right outer ankle, sweeping both feet cleanly together across the mat.
Kodokan Key Point: Floating timing — sweeping both feet when Uke's body weight is unweighted between steps. Repeated on the left side.
Ashi-wazaLateral SlideDual Foot Sweep
08
支釣込足 Sasae-tsurikomi-ashi "Propping and Drawing Ankle Throw"

Tori retreats three steps in Ayumi-ashi, drawing Uke into forward steps. Tori steps to the side, props the sole of the foot against Uke's ankle, and rotates Uke in a wheeling motion.

1.Tori retreats in natural walking steps (Ayumi-ashi) while Uke advances.
2.On the third step, Tori steps the left foot diagonally back-left, pulling Uke's right sleeve and lifting the collar in a circular steering wheel motion.
3.Tori places the sole of the right foot just above Uke's right ankle, blocking the step while driving Uke over the propped foot.
Kodokan Key Point: Propping, not kicking — the foot acts as a stationary barrier while upper body rotation completes the throw. Repeated on the left side.
Ashi-wazaAnkle PropCircular Wheel
09
内股 Uchi-mata "Inner Thigh Throw"

Moving in circular walking steps, Tori draws Uke forward, enters deeply between Uke's legs, and reaps upward against the inner thigh with the back of the leg.

1.Tori leads Uke in a large circular trajectory, breaking Uke's balance forward onto the right toes.
2.Tori pivots 180 degrees, planting the left support foot directly between Uke's feet while pulling the lapel and sleeve high.
3.Tori kicks the right leg high up between Uke's thighs, lifting the inner left thigh to launch Uke forward.
Kodokan Key Point: Center reap — the reaping leg acts as an elevator between Uke's thighs combined with strong forward head rotation. Repeated on the left side.
Ashi-wazaInner Thigh ReapHigh Kick
10
巴投 Tomoe-nage "Circular Throw / Stomach Throw"

Uke steps forward pushing in defensive posture. Tori steps deep between Uke's feet, drops flat onto the back, places the foot on Uke's lower abdomen, and flips Uke overhead.

1.Uke advances with pushing pressure in Jigotai; Tori retreats two steps.
2.On the third step, Tori slides the left foot deep between Uke's feet and drops straight down to the mat close to Uke's heels.
3.Tori places the sole of the right foot on Uke's lower abdomen and extends the leg upward while pulling both sleeves, projecting Uke in a full arc overhead.
Kodokan Key Point: Deep entry drop — Tori must sit as close to Uke's heels as possible before falling back to ensure a high, safe overhead arc. Repeated on the left side.
Ma-sutemi-wazaRear SacrificeOverhead Arc
11
裏投 Ura-nage "Rear Throw / Suplex Throw"

Uke advances with an overhead hammer-fist strike. Tori steps deep behind Uke's side, wraps both arms around Uke's torso, arches backward, and hurls Uke over the shoulder.

1.Uke steps forward with the right foot, raising the fist to strike down at Tori's forehead.
2.Tori ducks beneath the blow, stepping the left foot deep behind Uke's left heel, wrapping the right arm around Uke's waist and left hand on the abdomen.
3.Tori drops weight backward, thrusts the hips upward into a full bridge, and throws Uke backward over the left shoulder.
Kodokan Key Point: Full pelvic extension — Tori's backward sacrifice uses explosive hip drive to launch Uke safely over the shoulder. Repeated on the left side.
Ma-sutemi-wazaStrike CounterRear Arch
12
隅返 Sumi-gaeshi "Corner Reversal"

From a deep Jigotai position, Tori reaches over Uke's back to grip the belt, drops backward onto the mat, and hooks the instep inside Uke's inner thigh to flip Uke over the corner.

1.Both partners engage in defensive posture (Jigotai); Tori reaches the right arm deep over Uke's left shoulder to grip the rear belt.
2.Tori steps back with the left foot, sits straight down to the tatami, and slides the right instep under Uke's left inner thigh.
3.Tori rolls onto the back, lifting Uke's inner thigh with the instep and pulling the belt to flip Uke over the corner (Sumi).
Kodokan Key Point: Belt grip & instep hook — the instep drives upward in the direction of Uke's diagonal corner balance. Repeated on the left side.
Ma-sutemi-wazaBelt GripDiagonal Corner Flip
13
横掛 Yoko-gake "Side Hook Throw"

Tori retreats three steps in Ayumi-ashi. On the third step, Tori draws Uke onto the right heel, hooks the outside of Uke's right ankle with the sole of the foot, and drops sideways to the mat.

1.Tori retreats three natural walking steps (Ayumi-ashi) while leading Uke forward.
2.On the third step, Tori pulls strongly with both hands to fix Uke's weight completely onto the right heel.
3.Tori applies the sole of the left foot to the outside of Uke's right ankle and sacrifices body balance flat to the left side, sweeping Uke down.
Kodokan Key Point: Lateral sacrifice — Tori's side drop and ankle sweep must occur in one instantaneous, synchronized motion. Repeated on the left side.
Yoko-sutemi-wazaSide SacrificeAnkle Block Sweep
14
横車 Yoko-guruma "Side Wheel"

Uke steps in to strike Tori's forehead. Tori ducks under, reaches around Uke's waist as if to attempt Ura-nage, but drops onto the side flank and slides the leg between Uke's thighs to wheel Uke over.

1.Uke steps forward with the right foot, delivering a downward hammer-fist blow to Tori's forehead.
2.Tori ducks under, wrapping both arms around Uke's waist as Uke bends forward defensively to block Ura-nage.
3.Tori slides the right leg deep between Uke's legs, falls sideways onto the left hip, and wheels Uke in a large diagonal circle.
Kodokan Key Point: Countering defensive bend — capitalizing when Uke bends forward to resist a rear throw. Repeated on the left side.
Yoko-sutemi-wazaSide WheelLeg Insertion
15
浮技 Uki-waza "Floating Technique"

The final technique of Nage-no-Kata. Uke pushes forward in deep Jigotai. Tori retreats, breaks Uke's balance to the forward-right corner, and drops onto the left side while extending the leg to project Uke.

1.Both assume deep defensive posture (Jigotai); Uke drives forward with heavy pushing pressure.
2.On the third retreating step, Tori steps the left foot wide to the side and pulls Uke's right arm across in a large diagonal arc.
3.Tori drops onto the left side on the mat, extending the left leg straight, pulling Uke into a wide floating forward roll to conclude the kata.
Kodokan Key Point: Grand floating projection — Tori's side sacrifice creates a deep diagonal void, floating Uke in a sweeping circular fall. Repeated on the left side.
Yoko-sutemi-wazaJigotai CounterKata Finale

Principles & Etiquette

Kodokan Reihō & Throwing Principles

Nage-no-Kata embodies the complete dynamic mechanics of Kodokan standing judo.

Kuzushi, Tsukuri, Kake

Every throw requires the flawless sequence: breaking balance (Kuzushi), fitting the body without resistance (Tsukuri), and executing the throw cleanly with full control (Kake).

Right & Left Symmetry

All 15 techniques must be performed first on the right side (Migi) and then on the left side (Hidari), totaling 30 throws to ensure balanced bilateral body development.

Opening & Closing Reihō

Partners stand 5.5m apart (6 tatami lengths). They bow standing (Ritsurei) to Joseki, turn to face each other, assume Seiza for seated bow (Zarei), rise, and step forward with the left foot.
