Introduction

What is Seiryoku Zen'yo Kokumin Taiiku?

Seiryoku Zen'yo Kokumin Taiiku (精力善用国民体育) was created by Jigoro Kano in 1924 as a comprehensive physical education, self-defense, and wellness system designed for the entire nation (all ages and genders), whether practicing on tatami or on wooden floors.

The kata is composed of 50 total movement sequences structured across two primary divisions:
1. Tandoku-dōsa (単独動作 — Solo Practice, 29 movements): Systematic practice of striking techniques (Atemi-waza) — fists, chops, elbow strikes, and kicks executed while stationary (Dai-ikkyo) and with stepping footwork (Dai-nikyo).
2. Sotai-dōsa (相対動作 — Paired Practice, 21 movements): Divided into Kime-shiki (極式 — 10 decisive self-defense forms) against grabs and strikes, and Ju-shiki (柔式 — 11 yielding forms) emphasizing fluid, circular momentum control.

This kata represents the physical realization of Kano’s foundational doctrine: Seiryoku Zen'yo (Maximum Efficiency of Mind and Body) for personal health, combative readiness, and moral development.

DIVISION 01 — 29 MOVEMENTS

Tandoku-dōsa (Solo Practice)

単独動作 — Solo Calisthenics & Atemi-waza

Sotai-dōsa (Paired Practice)

相対動作 — Paired Self-Defense Applications

Practiced with a partner to apply decisive counter-strikes and fluid yielding controls against realistic attacks.

  • Ju-shiki (Yielding Forms) 11 Flowing Control Exercises
  • Ryote-dori & Mune-oshi Grip & Shove Defense

Step-by-Step

Technique Breakdown

Explore the complete Kodokan curriculum across Tandoku-dōsa, Kime-shiki, and Ju-shiki.

01
五方向突 Goho-ate "Five Direction Strikes (Stationary)"

Standing in Shizen-hontai, the practitioner executes five distinct fist strikes to primary target directions: Front (Mae-ate), Right (Migi-ate), Left (Hidari-ate), Rear (Ushiro-ate), and Overhead/Sky (Ue-ate).

1.Mae-ate (Forward): Thrust the right fist straight forward to solar plexus height, rotating the fist palm-down at impact.
2.Migi-ate & Hidari-ate (Sides): Thrust the fist sharply to the right and left flanks, turning the head toward the target.
3.Ushiro-ate & Ue-ate (Rear & Overhead): Drive a rear elbow/fist directly backward, followed by a straight vertical upward punch toward the sky.
Kodokan Key Point: Core alignment — maintain an upright spine and breathe out with crisp focus (Kime) on each extension.
Tandoku-dōsaStationaryFist AtemiFive Directions
02
大刀打・小刀打 Daito-uchi & Shoto-uchi "Long Sword Chop & Short Sword Chop"

Executing powerful outer hand-blade strikes (Tegatana). Daito-uchi uses a wide, sweeping outer sword-edge chop; Shoto-uchi uses a compact, rapid chopping strike.

1.Raise the right hand open with fingers pressed together and thumb tucked (Tegatana).
2.Execute a wide diagonal downward chop across the front (Daito-uchi) engaging hip rotation.
3.Snap the wrist outward in a fast horizontal chop (Shoto-uchi) aimed at the opponent's temple/neck.
Kodokan Key Point: Tegatana blade — strike with the little-finger edge (radial/ulnar border) of the palm, tensing at the point of contact.
Tandoku-dōsaStationaryHand Chop (Tegatana)
03
蹴上・後蹴 Ke-age & Ushiro-geri "Front Snap Kick & Rear Thrust Kick"

Solo kicking exercises developing balance on one leg, hip extension, and accuracy. Ke-age snaps upward to groin/solar plexus height; Ushiro-geri thrusts the heel straight backward.

1.Ke-age: Raise the knee and snap the right instep/ball of the foot straight upward to groin height, returning smoothly to stance.
2.Ushiro-geri: Look over the shoulder, drive the right heel straight backward into an imaginary attacker's abdomen, and recover without stumbling.
Kodokan Key Point: Standing base — keep the supporting knee slightly flexed with toes gripping the mat for balance.
Tandoku-dōsaStationaryLeg Kicking Atemi
04
鏡磨 Kagami-migaki "Mirror Polishing Exercise"

The famous mirror-polishing movement. The practitioner steps forward and backward while moving both open palms in large, overlapping circular orbits, cultivating fluid shoulder mobility, core breathing, and circular deflection.

1.Step forward with the right foot, making large outward circular sweeping motions with both open palms as if polishing a large round mirror.
2.Synchronize the expansion and contraction of the chest with natural deep abdominal breathing (Kokyu).
3.Step backward in Suri-ashi, reversing the circular direction of the palms smoothly without interruption.
Kodokan Key Point: Circular fluidity — relax the shoulders and let the circular palm paths flow continuously from the hips and breath.
Tandoku-dōsaMovingCircular MobilityHealth & Breathing
05
左右交互突 Sayu-kogo-tsuki "Alternating Left & Right Moving Punches"

Advancing and retreating across the tatami in natural walking steps (Ayumi-ashi), delivering alternating straight punches with hip rotation and crisp recoil.

1.Step the right foot forward, driving a crisp right straight punch (Mae-tsuki) with left hand chambered at the hip.
2.Step the left foot forward, switching the hips and driving a left straight punch.
3.Retreat backward in Ayumi-ashi, executing matching straight punches on each retreating step.
Kodokan Key Point: Stepping synchronization — impact occurs precisely at the moment the stepping foot touches the tatami.
Tandoku-dōsaMovingMoving PunchesAyumi-ashi
06
両手取 Ryote-dori "Both Wrists Seized (Kime-shiki)"

Uke grabs both of Tori's wrists from the front. Tori opens the arms outward, delivers an instant right front kick (Mae-geri) to Uke's groin, steps back, and applies a double-wrist twist (Kote-gaeshi) to force Uke down.

1.Uke steps in and firmly seizes both of Tori's wrists.
2.Tori spreads both hands outward, unbalancing Uke forward, and delivers a right front snap kick to Uke's groin (Kinteki).
3.Tori steps back with the left foot, twists Uke's right wrist outward, and forces Uke to submit on the mat.
Kodokan Key Point: Immediate kick atemi — kick immediately upon contact to break Uke's posture before applying the wrist lock.
Sotai-dōsaKime-shikiWrist EscapeKote-gaeshi
07
胸押 Mune-oshi "Chest Push Defense (Kime-shiki)"

Uke steps forward pushing Tori's chest. Tori covers Uke's hand, delivers a rising palm-heel strike (Teisho) to Uke's chin, wraps Uke's arm, and forces Uke down in an armlock (Waki-gatame).

1.Uke steps forward placing the right palm firmly against Tori's chest to push.
2.Tori traps Uke's right hand with the left palm, strikes Uke's chin with a right palm heel (Teisho), and steps back-left.
3.Tori clamps Uke's extended right elbow under the armpit, forcing Uke prone onto the tatami.
Kodokan Key Point: Chin strike & armlock — snap Uke's head back with Teisho before transitioning into Waki-gatame.
Sotai-dōsaKime-shikiChin StrikeWaki-gatame
08
突掛 Tsuki-kake "Straight Punch Defense (Kime-shiki)"

Uke steps in with a straight right punch to Tori's face. Tori steps back-left (Tai-sabaki), parries Uke's punch, drives a right elbow strike into Uke's ribs/solar plexus, and throws Uke with Osoto-gari.

1.Uke advances with the right foot, throwing a straight punch at Tori's face.
2.Tori steps diagonally off the attack line, parries Uke's wrist with the left hand, and strikes Uke's ribs with the right elbow (Hiji-ate).
3.Tori steps deep behind Uke's right leg and sweeps Uke down with Osoto-gari.
Kodokan Key Point: Off-line parry & elbow strike — step off the centerline while simultaneously countering with the elbow strike.
Sotai-dōsaKime-shikiPunch DefenseOsoto-gari
09
突出 Tsuki-dashi "Thrust Out (Ju-shiki)"

Uke advances pushing both hands into Tori's chest. Tori yields by stepping back, guides Uke's hands in a smooth downward circular arc, and unbalances Uke forward onto the toes (identical principle to Ju-no-Kata).

1.Uke steps forward driving both open hands toward Tori's chest.
2.Tori steps back with the left foot, placing hands lightly on Uke's forearms without resisting.
3.Tori leads Uke's arms in a circular downward arc, pulling Uke forward into deep Kuzushi.
Kodokan Key Point: Harmonious yield — do not block; guide Uke's momentum along a circular path to create effortless off-balancing.
Sotai-dōsaJu-shikiCircular YieldKuzushi
10
肩廻 Kata-mawashi "Shoulder Turn (Ju-shiki)"

The final concluding paired exercise. Uke places hands on Tori's shoulders to rotate Tori. Tori pivots with the turn, absorbs the rotational force, and reverses the pivot to draw Uke into rearward unbalance.

1.Uke steps in and places both hands on Tori's shoulders, applying rotational torque to turn Tori.
2.Tori pivots smoothly on the left foot, turning with Uke's force through a quarter turn.
3.Tori reverses the pivot smoothly to the left, taking control of Uke's balance and drawing Uke backward into final Kuzushi.
Kodokan Key Point: Rotational redirection — flow seamlessly with Uke's turn and reverse direction at the moment of momentum transition.
Sotai-dōsaJu-shikiRotational PivotKata Finale

Philosophy & Wellness

Seiryoku Zen'yo & National Physical Culture

Jigoro Kano's grand vision for lifelong health, physical vigor, and moral development through judo principles.

Lifelong Health & Hygiene

Tandoku-dōsa was designed so that any person, regardless of age, gender, or mat availability, can train daily to maintain cardiovascular fitness, joint flexibility, and mental focus.

Integration of Atemi-waza

While striking is excluded from sporting Randori for safety, Seiryoku Zen'yo preserves the complete historical striking arsenal of classical Jujutsu within a safe, structured educational format.

Reihō & Protocol

All sessions begin and end with formal bows (Ritsurei at 30 degrees). Solo practice maintains a disciplined martial posture (Shizentai) with upright spine and controlled abdominal breathing.
