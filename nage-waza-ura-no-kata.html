Introduction

What is Nage-waza Ura-no-Kata?

Nage-waza Ura-no-Kata (投技裏の形), meaning "Forms of Reversing Throwing Techniques," represents the tactical reverse side (Ura) of standard throwing mechanics. Formalized by the legendary 10th Dan master Kyūzō Mifune, it systematically examines how an initiated throw can be turned against the attacker.

In standard Nage-no-Kata (the Omote or surface form), Uke initiates an attack and Tori cleanly executes the primary throw. In Nage-waza Ura-no-Kata, Uke initiates a legitimate throwing attempt with full commitment, whereupon Tori yields slightly, prevents completion through subtle body movement (Tai-sabaki), and counters (Kaeshi-waza) at the exact moment of transition.

The kata demonstrates advanced principles of Go-no-Sen (countering after the attack begins) and Sen-no-Sen (countering at the exact inception of the attack), emphasizing non-resistance, soft balance shifts, and precise timing without brute-force blocking.

Comprising 15 paired techniques across hand (Te-waza), foot (Ashi-waza), and hip (Koshi-waza) categories, it remains one of the pinnacle studies of high-level intuitive judo (Myōwaza).

Structure

The Three Classified Sets

The kata is structured into three groups of five counter-techniques, classified by the primary mechanism of the initiated attack and its corresponding reversal.

SET 01

Te-waza Counters

手技裏 — Hand Throw Reversals

Focuses on countering shoulder, hand, and frontal drop attacks by unbalancing Uke around their own rotation axis or executing sacrifice throws.

  • 1. Uki-otoshi → Tai-otoshi
  • 2. Seoi-nage → Yoko-guruma
  • 3. Kata-guruma → Sumi-gaeshi
  • 4. Tai-otoshi → Ko-tsuri-goshi
  • 5. Obi-otoshi → O-guruma
SET 02

Ashi-waza Counters

足技裏 — Foot Throw Reversals

Demands lightning timing and light footwork to evade sweeps, reapings, and leg entries, turning momentum into instant aerial throws.

  • 6. Okuri-ashi-harai → Tsubame-gaeshi
  • 7. Ko-uchi-gari → Hiza-guruma
  • 8. O-uchi-gari → O-uchi-gaeshi
  • 9. Sasae-tsurikomi-ashi → Sumi-otoshi
  • 10. Uchi-mata → Tai-otoshi
SET 03

Koshi-waza Counters

腰技裏 — Hip Throw Reversals

Deals with heavy turning hip entries, using rear lifts, straddling shifts, and sutemi-waza drops to reverse close-quarter hip power.

  • 11. Hane-goshi → Kari-gaeshi
  • 12. Harai-goshi → Ushiro-goshi
  • 13. Hane-goshi → Utsuri-goshi
  • 14. Uki-goshi → Yoko-wakare
  • 15. O-goshi → Ippon-seoi-nage

Step-by-Step

Technique Breakdown

Detailed breakdown of attack initiation by Uke and execution of the counter-throw (Kaeshi-waza) by Tori.

01
浮落 → 体落 Uki-otoshi → Tai-otoshi "Floating Drop countered by Body Drop"

Uke steps back dynamically attempting a floating hand drop (Uki-otoshi). Tori resists being pulled downward, steps through with circular footwork, extends the leg across Uke's path, and executes Tai-otoshi.

1.Both partners engage in standard right grip (Migi-kumi). Uke retreats stepping back in Tsugi-ashi, pulling Tori into Uki-otoshi.
2.As Uke drops to one knee to complete the hand drop, Tori keeps upright posture and avoids the downward vacuum.
3.Tori advances the right foot across Uke’s committed front stance, extending the right leg to block Uke's shin.
4.Tori pulls strongly with the tsurite and hikite, driving Uke cleanly over the outstretched leg in Tai-otoshi.
Mifune Key Point: Upright preservation — rather than collapsing into Uke's pulling arc, Tori floats over the drop and converts Uke's pulling momentum into rotational torque.
Te-wazaHand CounterTai-otoshi
02
背負投 → 横車 Seoi-nage → Yoko-guruma "Shoulder Throw countered by Side Wheel"

Uke turns in deeply for a right Seoi-nage. Tori leans over Uke’s right shoulder, inserts the left arm around Uke's waist, slides a leg between Uke's legs, and drops to execute Yoko-guruma.

1.Uke steps in and pivots deeply with low hips to execute a classical Seoi-nage.
2.Tori sinks their center of gravity, wrapping the left arm around Uke's chest or waist to suppress the lift.
3.Tori slides the right leg deep between Uke's legs, sliding down onto the left side/back in Sutemi-waza fashion.
4.Tori rotates their entire torso, wheeling Uke diagonally over their body in a large Yoko-guruma arc.
Mifune Key Point: Close attachment — Tori must weld their torso to Uke's back before sliding into the sacrifice drop, leaving no space for Uke's shoulder lever.
Te-wazaSutemi-waza CounterYoko-guruma
03
肩車 → 隅返 Kata-guruma → Sumi-gaeshi "Shoulder Wheel countered by Corner Reversal"

Uke drops low and drives in to load Tori onto the shoulders for Kata-guruma. Tori wraps Uke's upper body with a deep overhook, falls backward, and flips Uke with Sumi-gaeshi.

1.Uke drops their stance deeply and shoots under Tori's center, grabbing Tori's inner right thigh for Kata-guruma.
2.Before Uke can stand and hoist, Tori locks an overhook around Uke's upper back and neck.
3.Tori hooks an instep inside Uke's thigh and commits their weight backward directly to the mat.
4.Tori elevates with the hooked foot, launching Uke over the top into a spectacular Sumi-gaeshi.
Mifune Key Point: Overcoming the duck-under — Tori utilizes Uke's downward head trajectory to accelerate the rearward sacrifice flip.
Te-wazaSutemi CounterSumi-gaeshi
04
体落 → 小釣込腰 Tai-otoshi → Ko-tsuri-goshi "Body Drop countered by Minor Lifting Hip"

Uke steps across to extend the leg for Tai-otoshi. Tori steps over the outstretched obstruction leg, attaches hip-to-hip, grips the back of the belt, and throws Uke over the hip with Ko-tsuri-goshi.

1.Uke enters rapidly, extending the right leg across Tori's right ankle to set the barrier for Tai-otoshi.
2.Tori steps lightly over Uke's outstretched leg with the right foot, neutralizing the obstruction.
3.Tori slides the right hand deep around Uke's back to grip the rear belt, inserting their hip directly under Uke's pelvis.
4.Tori hoists upward using the belt grip and pivots the hips to throw Uke forward-flat in Ko-tsuri-goshi.
Mifune Key Point: Step-over timing — evading the blocking leg without pausing, flowing directly into hip engagement.
Te-wazaObstruction EvasionKo-tsuri-goshi
05
帯落 → 大輪 Obi-otoshi → O-guruma "Belt Drop countered by Large Wheel"

Uke reaches across Tori’s front to seize the belt and drive into Obi-otoshi. Tori uses body evasion, reaches high over Uke’s back, extends the right leg across Uke’s thighs, and wheels Uke in O-guruma.

1.Uke steps in laterally with low hips, reaching around Tori's waist to secure the belt for Obi-otoshi.
2.Tori pivots back-right, denying Uke the close lever and maintaining shoulder elevation.
3.Tori establishes a dominant high back grip, pivoting on the left foot to extend the right leg horizontally across Uke's thigh/pelvis line.
4.Tori executes a powerful rotary fulcrum throw, sending Uke revolving over the leg in O-guruma.
Mifune Key Point: Rotary fulcrum — turning an incoming linear belt scoop into a wide circular wheeling throw.
Te-wazaLarge WheelO-guruma
06
送足払 → 燕返 Okuri-ashi-harai → Tsubame-gaeshi "Foot Sweep countered by Swallow's Flight Reversal"

Uke moves sideways in Tsugi-ashi and sweeps both of Tori's feet with Okuri-ashi-harai. Tori lifts the targeted foot slightly to let the sweep pass beneath, then instantly sweeps Uke's supporting leg with Tsubame-gaeshi.

1.Both judoka move sideways in lateral sliding steps (Tsugi-ashi) across the mat.
2.Uke times the step and sweeps with the left sole toward Tori's ankles for Okuri-ashi-harai.
3.Tori senses the sweep, stepping/floating the leading foot lightly over Uke's sweeping foot without touching.
4.As Uke overextends with the missed sweep, Tori immediately sweeps Uke's standing foot with a counter foot sweep (Tsubame-gaeshi).
Mifune Key Point: Feather-light foot lift — Tori avoids jumping; rather, Tori unweights the foot just enough for Uke's sole to slide underneath cleanly.
Ashi-wazaDe-ashi TimingTsubame-gaeshi
07
小内刈 → 膝車 Ko-uchi-gari → Hiza-guruma "Minor Inner Reap countered by Knee Wheel"

Uke steps in to hook Tori’s right heel with Ko-uchi-gari. Tori withdraws the threatened foot in a light arc, draws Uke forward onto that side, and blocks Uke’s supporting knee with Hiza-guruma.

1.Uke steps forward driving the right foot to clip Tori's trailing right heel from the inside in Ko-uchi-gari.
2.Tori instantly pulls back the right foot in a subtle circular arc, evading the reaping foot.
3.Tori applies strong steering-wheel rotation with both hands, drawing Uke's weight forward over the advancing leg.
4.Tori places the left sole against Uke's right knee cap and wheels Uke cleanly to the mat in Hiza-guruma.
Mifune Key Point: Evasion into steering wheel — the pulling hands must activate simultaneously with the withdrawing foot to trap Uke's momentum.
Ashi-wazaInner Reap EvasionHiza-guruma
08
大内刈 → 大内返 O-uchi-gari → O-uchi-gaeshi "Major Inner Reap countered by Major Inner Counter"

Uke drives deeply into Tori's center to reap the inner left leg with O-uchi-gari. Tori yields backwards slightly, braces the hip, clamps Uke’s upper torso, and re-reaps Uke to the rear in O-uchi-gaeshi.

1.Uke opens Tori's posture and drives a deep right leg reap between Tori's legs for O-uchi-gari.
2.Tori maintains balance on the non-targeted foot, flexing the knee and unweighting the reaped leg slightly.
3.Tori shifts their center forward against Uke’s chest, wrapping Uke tightly with the tsurite hand.
4.Tori reverses the leg hook leverage and drives Uke straight back down over Uke's supporting heel in O-uchi-gaeshi.
Mifune Key Point: Hip re-anchoring — Tori absorbs the initial chest-to-chest clash, then immediately uses superior body alignment to dominate the inner reap vector.
Ashi-wazaReap vs ReapO-uchi-gaeshi
09
支釣込足 → 隅落 Sasae-tsurikomi-ashi → Sumi-otoshi "Propping Foot countered by Corner Drop"

Uke steps back and blocks Tori’s front ankle with Sasae-tsurikomi-ashi. Tori resists being drawn forward, steps past the propping foot, and applies a corner hand-drop (Sumi-otoshi) to Uke’s rear diagonal.

1.Uke retreats and sets the left sole against Tori's right ankle, pulling with a high lifting sleeve action.
2.Tori lifts the right foot over the prop, stepping firmly on the outside of Uke's stance.
3.With Uke balanced on a single supporting foot, Tori drives the right hand backward diagonally while depressing the left.
4.Tori drops Uke to the rear corner without touching Uke with legs (pure Sumi-otoshi).
Mifune Key Point: Air throw precision (Kuki-nage) — Tori executes the counter purely through hand coordination and unbalancing (Kuzushi) without leg contact.
Ashi-wazaCorner DropSumi-otoshi
10
内股 → 体落 Uchi-mata → Tai-otoshi "Inner Thigh Throw countered by Body Drop"

Uke turns in deep and kicks up between Tori’s thighs with a right Uchi-mata. Tori dodges the inner reaping leg by shifting the hips, steps around to the flank, and executes a sharp Tai-otoshi.

1.Uke enters explosively with deep hip penetration, lifting high between Tori's thighs for Uchi-mata.
2.Tori jumps/shifts the hips slightly to the left (Tai-sabaki), clearing the path of Uke's rising inner leg.
3.As Uke's kicking leg misses and Uke stands on one foot, Tori swiftly pivots and extends the right leg across Uke's supporting post.
4.Tori twists both hands downward across the chest, driving Uke over the barrier leg in Tai-otoshi.
Mifune Key Point: Clearing the kick — slipping the groin away from Uke's thigh lift while instantly pinning Uke's single standing leg with Tai-otoshi.
Ashi-wazaFlank TransitionTai-otoshi
11
跳腰 → 刈返 Hane-goshi → Kari-gaeshi "Springing Hip countered by Reaping Reversal"

Uke springs upward with the hip and bent leg for Hane-goshi. Tori drops their weight, evades the spring, reaches around Uke’s supporting leg, and reaps Uke backward with Kari-gaeshi.

1.Uke turns in, placing the right hip and outer thigh against Tori's leg, springing upward for Hane-goshi.
2.Tori lowers the center of gravity, sinking their hips and leaning slightly around Uke's side to absorb the spring.
3.Tori reaches their right leg or arm behind Uke's single weight-bearing left leg.
4.Tori reaps out the supporting leg from behind, dumping Uke straight onto the back in Kari-gaeshi.
Mifune Key Point: Sinking under the spring — absorbing the upward lift by lowering the hips before reaping the exposed supporting base.
Koshi-wazaSpring CounterKari-gaeshi
12
払腰 → 後腰 Harai-goshi → Ushiro-goshi "Sweeping Hip countered by Rear Hip Throw"

Uke pivots and sweeps the outer hip and leg for Harai-goshi. Tori drops their hips deeply, wraps both arms tightly around Uke’s waist from the rear, hoists Uke off the mat, and drops them back with Ushiro-goshi.

1.Uke turns in with strong forward pull, loading the hip and sweeping the right leg back for Harai-goshi.
2.Tori bends both knees deeply into a solid base (Jigotai), preventing Uke from completing the hip sweep.
3.Tori clasps both arms around Uke's abdomen, driving their hips upward into Uke's lower back to lift Uke completely.
4.Tori steps back with one leg, guiding Uke down firmly to the mat on Uke's back in Ushiro-goshi.
Mifune Key Point: Hip block and elevation — sinking below Uke's belt line is critical to generating the explosive upward lift required for Ushiro-goshi.
Koshi-wazaRear LiftUshiro-goshi
13
跳腰 → 移腰 Hane-goshi → Utsuri-goshi "Springing Hip countered by Shifting Hip Throw"

Uke attacks with a second variation of Hane-goshi. Tori absorbs the spring, hoists Uke into the air with the belly/hips, switches their own hip across to the opposite side, and throws Uke forward with Utsuri-goshi.

1.Uke steps in and lifts with the right leg and hip in Hane-goshi.
2.Tori absorbs the impact by squatting, wraps the left arm around Uke's waist, and elevates Uke directly upward with the pelvic drive.
3.While Uke is suspended in mid-air, Tori shifts (switches) their left hip underneath Uke's front.
4.Tori pivots forward, throwing Uke in a smooth forward hip throw (Utsuri-goshi).
Mifune Key Point: Mid-air hip shift — the transition of Tori's hip from the rear to the front must take place at the apex of the lift without hesitation.
Koshi-wazaHip TransitionUtsuri-goshi
14
浮腰 → 横分 Uki-goshi → Yoko-wakare "Floating Hip countered by Side Separation"

Uke wraps the waist for a half-hip throw (Uki-goshi). Tori steps around Uke's hip, wraps their arm over Uke's shoulder, and drops sideways to the mat in Yoko-wakare, pulling Uke straight down over them.

1.Uke steps across with a shallow hip entry and wraps the left arm around Tori's back for Uki-goshi.
2.Tori slides their hips slightly back and steps the left foot deep to Uke's left front flank.
3.Tori reaches high over Uke's back, pulls Uke's sleeve firmly, and extends both legs sideways while dropping flat to the floor.
4.Tori separates their body downward, hurling Uke over their side in a classic Yoko-wakare drop.
Mifune Key Point: Side sacrifice drop — Tori uses the downward pull of their own falling mass to flip Uke directly over the lateral plane.
Koshi-wazaSutemi CounterYoko-wakare
15
大腰 → 一本背負投 O-goshi → Ippon-seoi-nage "Major Hip Throw countered by One-Arm Shoulder Throw"

The grand finale. Uke steps deep and wraps the waist for a powerful O-goshi. Tori slides past Uke’s hip rotation, clamps Uke’s reaching right arm under the right armpit, and executes an explosive counter Ippon-seoi-nage.

1.Uke steps in deeply with the right foot, wrapping the right arm around Tori's waist to hoist with O-goshi.
2.Tori steps forward-left around Uke’s right hip, slipping inside the wrapping arm before the lift is engaged.
3.Tori traps Uke's right arm deeply in their right elbow crook/armpit, spinning 180 degrees beneath Uke's chest.
4.Tori extends their legs and bows forward, throwing Uke cleanly over the shoulder in a definitive Ippon-seoi-nage.
Mifune Key Point: Inside spin reversal — turning Uke's encircling arm into the exact lever for an instantaneous forward shoulder throw.

Principles & Tactical Theory

The Science of Kaeshi-waza

Kyūzō Mifune's counter-throw pedagogy rests on three foundational pillars of dynamic reaction.

Go-no-Sen (後の先)

The principle of responding after the opponent initiates. Instead of opposing the attack with muscular resistance, Tori allows the attack to enter, yields just enough to escape the point of leverage, and counters as Uke is extended.

Sen-no-Sen (先の先)

Taking the initiative simultaneously with Uke’s attack. Tori detects the inception of Uke's technique and enters the counter-throw at the exact instant Uke commits weight, overtaking Uke's momentum before balance is set.

Tai-sabaki & Myōwaza (妙技)

Fluid, spherical body motion without stiffness. Reversing throws cannot be achieved by locking the joints; Tori must act like a spinning top or rubber sphere, redirecting vectors around an unmovable center of gravity.
