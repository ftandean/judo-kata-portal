Introduction

What is Ju-no-Kata?

Ju-no-Kata (柔の形), meaning "Forms of Gentleness," is one of the most philosophical and physically expressive kata in Kodokan Judo[cite: 2]. Created by Jigoro Kano in the late 19th century, it was designed to teach the fundamental principle of Ju — yielding to overcome force[cite: 2].

Unlike Nage-no-Kata (throwing) or Katame-no-Kata (grappling), Ju-no-Kata contains no actual throws or groundwork submissions[cite: 2]. Instead, it is performed as a smooth, continuous sequence of fifteen paired interactions between Tori (the defender) and Uke (the attacker)[cite: 2].

The movements emphasize upright natural posture (Shizentai), smooth circular redirection (Tai-sabaki), controlled extension, and mutual unbalancing (Kuzushi)[cite: 2]. Because it can be practiced safely by judoka of all ages, it serves as both physical conditioning and deep technical education[cite: 2].

Both partners maintain constant physical and mental contact throughout, making Ju-no-Kata an exemplary physical demonstration of Seiryoku Zen'yo (Maximum Efficiency) and Jita Kyoei (Mutual Welfare and Benefit)[cite: 2].

Structure

The Three Sets

Ju-no-Kata is divided into three teachings (Kyo), each containing five techniques that progress from upright basic yields to dynamic full-body unbalancing[cite: 2].

SET 01

Dai-ikkyo

第一教 — The First Teaching

Introduces the core concepts of yielding to direct push and pull forces while preserving natural posture, circular movement, and forward/rear kuzushi[cite: 2].

  • 1. Tsuki-dashi Thrust Out
  • 2. Kata-oshi Shoulder Push
  • 3. Ryōte-dori Both Wrists Seized
  • 4. Kata-mawashi Shoulder Turn
  • 5. Ago-oshi Chin Push
SET 02

Dai-nikyo

第二教 — The Second Teaching

Explores multi-directional attacks and evasive body turns (Tai-sabaki), featuring strikes, single-wrist grabs, and large upward extensions[cite: 2].

  • 6. Kiri-oroshi Downward Cut
  • 7. Ryōkata-oshi Both Shoulders Push
  • 8. Naname-uchi Diagonal Strike
  • 9. Katate-dori One Wrist Seized
  • 10. Katate-age One Arm Raised
SET 03

Dai-sankyo

第三教 — The Third Teaching

The most dynamic set, featuring powerful linear thrusts, belt grips, and full-body yielding maneuvers requiring refined coordination[cite: 2].

  • 11. Obi-tori Belt Seized
  • 12. Mune-oshi Chest Push
  • 13. Tsuki-age Uppercut Thrust
  • 14. Uchi-oroshi Downward Blow
  • 15. Ryōgan-tsuki Both Eyes Thrust

Step-by-Step

Technique Breakdown

Each technique is detailed according to the official Kodokan Judo textbook standard, illustrating attack momentum, yielding vectors, and kuzushi[cite: 2].

01
突出 Tsuki-dashi "Thrust Out"

Uke steps forward thrusting both open palms into Tori's chest[cite: 2]. Tori yields by stepping back, leading Uke's hands downward in a circular arc to draw Uke off balance forward[cite: 2].

1.Both partners stand facing each other in Shizen-hontai at standard distance (approx. 2.7m / 3 tatami)[cite: 2].
2.Uke steps forward with the right foot, thrusting both open hands toward Tori's chest[cite: 2].
3.Tori steps back with the left foot, places both hands lightly on Uke's forearms, and leads Uke in a smooth circular arc downward[cite: 2].
4.Uke is drawn forward onto the toes into forward kuzushi, then both smoothly return to Shizentai[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Circular redirection — Tori does not push back against Uke's momentum, but harmonizes and extends Uke's forward energy[cite: 2].
Dai-ikkyoLinear PushCircular Yield
02
肩押 Kata-oshi "Shoulder Push"

Uke places the right hand on Tori's left shoulder and pushes[cite: 2]. Tori yields diagonally back to the right, letting Uke overextend, and guides Uke's elbow to off-balance them forward-right[cite: 2].

1.Uke steps forward with the right foot, placing the right palm against Tori's left shoulder[cite: 2].
2.Tori steps back diagonally to the right-rear with the right foot, rotating the torso slightly to absorb the force[cite: 2].
3.Tori places the left hand under Uke's right elbow, guiding the forward momentum to bring Uke's weight over the front right foot[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Tai-sabaki — Tori's shoulder recedes smoothly while the hips rotate, avoiding rigid resistance[cite: 2].
Dai-ikkyoDiagonal StepElbow Guide
03
両手取 Ryōte-dori "Both Wrists Seized"

Uke grips both of Tori's wrists and pulls backward[cite: 2]. Tori steps forward into the pull, sweeping both arms upward in a large arc to break the grip and draw Uke forward onto the toes[cite: 2].

1.Uke steps back grasping both of Tori's wrists, pulling backward with steady force[cite: 2].
2.Tori follows the pull smoothly, stepping forward with the right foot[cite: 2].
3.Tori sweeps both arms upward in a wide circular arc overhead, breaking Uke's grip and elevating Uke onto their toes[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Stepping into the pull — Tori utilizes Uke's backward traction to accelerate forward and unbalance Uke upward[cite: 2].
Dai-ikkyoWrist ReleaseUpward Kuzushi
04
肩廻 Kata-mawashi "Shoulder Turn"

Uke places hands on Tori's shoulders and attempts to spin Tori[cite: 2]. Tori yields to the rotation, absorbs the momentum, and reverses the turn to bring Uke off balance to the rear[cite: 2].

1.Uke steps forward placing both hands on Tori's shoulders, applying rotational torque to turn Tori to the right[cite: 2].
2.Tori yields freely, pivoting smoothly on the left foot to complete a quarter turn[cite: 2].
3.As Uke's rotational force finishes, Tori reverses the pivot to the left, taking control and drawing Uke off balance rearward[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Rotational yielding — flowing with the circular attack and reversing direction at the exact point of inertia[cite: 2].
Dai-ikkyoRotational PivotDirection Reversal
05
顎押 Ago-oshi "Chin Push"

Uke pushes under Tori's chin with an upward palm[cite: 2]. Tori yields backward, grips Uke's wrist, and ducks underneath the arm with a 180-degree turn to reverse the leverage[cite: 2].

1.Uke advances with the right foot, driving the right palm upward beneath Tori's chin[cite: 2].
2.Tori steps back with the left foot, yielding to the upward tilt of the head without tensing[cite: 2].
3.Tori controls Uke's right wrist with both hands, turns under Uke's raised arm, and locks the joint to pull Uke forward[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Under-arm turn — escaping upward pressure through fluid body pivoting and joint alignment[cite: 2].
Dai-ikkyoChin PushBody Spin
06
切下 Kiri-oroshi "Downward Cut"

Uke raises clasped hands overhead and strikes downward toward Tori's head[cite: 2]. Tori steps diagonally forward-left to evade the line of attack, trapping and guiding Uke's wrists downward[cite: 2].

1.Uke raises hands overhead in a sword-cutting posture and steps forward, executing a downward blow[cite: 2].
2.Tori steps diagonally forward-left with the left foot (Tai-sabaki), letting the strike pass safely[cite: 2].
3.Tori controls Uke's descending wrists, drawing them in a forward arc to off-balance Uke forward[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Moving off the center line — stepping into the blind angle while controlling the descending momentum[cite: 2].
Dai-nikyoOverhead StrikeOff-Line Evasion
07
両肩押 Ryōkata-oshi "Both Shoulders Push"

Uke places both hands on Tori's shoulders and pushes forward[cite: 2]. Tori lowers the center of gravity, steps back, and sweeps both arms under Uke's elbows to lift and redirect[cite: 2].

1.Uke advances and presses firmly against both of Tori's shoulders with extended arms[cite: 2].
2.Tori sinks their hips in Shizentai and steps the right foot back, absorbing the linear drive[cite: 2].
3.Tori brings both hands upward beneath Uke's elbows, lifting and expanding to break Uke's posture[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Sinking the hips — absorbing a horizontal push by lowering the center of mass before redirecting upward[cite: 2].
Dai-nikyoShoulder PushElbow Lift
08
斜打 Naname-uchi "Diagonal Strike"

Uke steps forward with a diagonal right hand-strike to Tori's temple[cite: 2]. Tori ducks underneath the strike with a circular pivot, stepping inside to control Uke's center[cite: 2].

1.Uke steps forward with the right foot, delivering a diagonal chopping strike (Naname-uchi) toward Tori's left temple[cite: 2].
2.Tori ducks smoothly beneath the trajectory of the strike while stepping forward-right[cite: 2].
3.Tori comes up on Uke's right flank, controlling Uke's right arm and unbalancing Uke to the rear-diagonal[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Ducking under — ducking with fluid posture rather than bending excessively at the waist[cite: 2].
Dai-nikyoDiagonal ChopUnder-Strike Evasion
09
片手取 Katate-dori "One Wrist Seized"

Uke grabs Tori's right wrist with their left hand and pulls[cite: 2]. Tori steps forward with the pull, raising the captured arm in a circular arc to twist Uke's balance outward[cite: 2].

1.Uke reaches across with the left hand to seize Tori's right wrist and steps backward[cite: 2].
2.Tori steps forward into Uke's pull with the right foot[cite: 2].
3.Tori rotates the right wrist and raises the arm diagonally outward, unbalancing Uke over their heels[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Spiral arm rotation — rotating the wrist outward to loosen the grip while stepping in to control the center[cite: 2].
Dai-nikyoSingle WristSpiral Raise
10
片手挙 Katate-age "One Arm Raised"

Uke raises the right hand high as if to strike downward[cite: 2]. Tori steps deeply inside before the strike can develop, placing their palm under Uke's raised elbow to float Uke backward[cite: 2].

1.Uke raises the right arm high overhead, preparing to strike[cite: 2].
2.Tori steps in swiftly with the right foot, entering Uke's space before the blow descends (Sen-no-sen timing)[cite: 2].
3.Tori supports Uke's right elbow with the left palm, raising it higher and driving Uke onto their heels[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Entering at inception (Sen) — seizing the initiative before the opponent's strike reaches maximum acceleration[cite: 2].
Dai-nikyoPreemptive EntryElbow Lift
11
帯取 Obi-tori "Belt Seized"

Uke steps in and reaches around Tori's waist to seize the belt from the rear[cite: 2]. Tori pivots smoothly, slips out of the grip, and brings Uke off balance forward-right[cite: 2].

1.Uke steps forward with the right foot and reaches with the right arm around Tori's waist to grab the belt[cite: 2].
2.Tori turns the hips to the left, stepping back with the right foot to slip the waist out of Uke's reach[cite: 2].
3.Tori controls Uke's right arm, redirecting Uke's forward momentum into a deep forward kuzushi[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Hip evasion — turning the pelvis to deny the belt grip without bending over or bracing[cite: 2].
Dai-sankyoBelt GrabHip Escape
12
胸押 Mune-oshi "Chest Push"

Uke drives forward with both hands to push Tori's chest vigorously[cite: 2]. Tori yields in a deep rearward step, lowering their center of mass and drawing Uke into maximum extension[cite: 2].

1.Uke steps forward forcefully, placing both palms against Tori's chest with strong forward drive[cite: 2].
2.Tori takes a long sliding step backward with the left foot, absorbing the thrust cleanly[cite: 2].
3.Tori guides Uke's wrists downward and outward, leaving Uke completely overbalanced forward[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Total yielding — creating a vacuum that leaves the pusher unsupported over their forward leg[cite: 2].
Dai-sankyoHeavy PushDeep Step Back
13
突上 Tsuki-age "Uppercut Thrust"

Uke delivers a rising fist thrust toward Tori's chin[cite: 2]. Tori leans the head back slightly, controls Uke's rising wrist, and steps inside to control the elbow and balance[cite: 2].

1.Uke steps forward, driving a rising right uppercut (Tsuki-age) aimed at Tori's chin[cite: 2].
2.Tori yields by stepping back with the left foot and tilting the upper torso back just enough to clear the fist[cite: 2].
3.Tori secures Uke's right wrist and rotates the joint outward, locking Uke's arm in full extension[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Minimal evasion — yielding only the exact distance required to avoid the strike while maintaining counter-position[cite: 2].
Dai-sankyoUppercutJoint Extension
14
打下 Uchi-oroshi "Downward Blow"

Uke raises the right fist overhead and strikes downward[cite: 2]. Tori steps diagonally forward with the right foot, checks Uke's arm at the elbow, and guides Uke into a deep forward lunge[cite: 2].

1.Uke raises the right fist overhead and strikes downward toward Tori's forehead[cite: 2].
2.Tori steps forward-right (Tai-sabaki), moving inside the line of attack[cite: 2].
3.Tori guides Uke's striking arm downward past the hip, causing Uke to pitch forward onto the front foot[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Inside pivot — controlling the trajectory of the descending limb from the inside flank[cite: 2].
Dai-sankyoHammer BlowInside Control
15
両眼突 Ryōgan-tsuki "Both Eyes Thrust"

The final technique of Ju-no-Kata[cite: 2]. Uke thrusts the fingertips of both hands toward Tori's eyes[cite: 2]. Tori steps back, yields the head, and guides Uke's wrists in a sweeping outward arc[cite: 2].

1.Uke steps forward with the right foot, thrusting the fingertips of both hands toward Tori's eyes[cite: 2].
2.Tori steps back with the left foot, allowing the head and upper body to yield backward away from the fingertips[cite: 2].
3.Tori sweeps both hands upward and outward against Uke's forearms, opening Uke's posture and drawing them into a final forward kuzushi[cite: 2].
Kodokan Key Point: Grand circular finale — expanding the arms outward to fully open and neutralize Uke's final attack before returning to Shizen-hontai[cite: 2].
Dai-sankyoEye ThrustFinal Technique

Principles & Etiquette

Kodokan Principles & Reihō

Ju-no-Kata represents the physical manifestation of Judo's deepest philosophical doctrines[cite: 2].

Principle of Ju (柔)

The core concept of yielding to force rather than resisting head-on[cite: 2]. By moving with the opponent's momentum, one neutralizes destructive energy and controls balance with minimum effort[cite: 2].

Shizentai & Tai-sabaki

Every technique begins and ends in Shizen-hontai (natural posture)[cite: 2]. Body movement (Tai-sabaki) relies on circular hip rotation, keeping the spine erect and breathing centered in the tanden[cite: 2].

Reihō (Etiquette)

Partners begin standing 2.7m (approx. 3 tatami lengths) apart[cite: 2]. They perform Ritsurei toward Joseki, turn to face each other, perform Ritsurei, and begin with left-foot entry into Shizentai[cite: 2].
